Simulation log

Every CST run of the TX/RX isolation study, newest first: what was simulated, the result, and what it means. The current best numbers are on the status page.

Last updated 25 Oct 2026. Requirement: 70 dB isolation, i.e. coupling B ≤ −50.2 dB (definitions on the status page). Numbers are worst cases over the ±20° TX scan and 95–99.9 GHz. Run names in mono are for reference.

25 Oct 2026

running  N8wr → N8wr2

Radome cut above the wall: first attempt unstable, corrected run started

Model
4×4 + 4×4, 5 mm gap, radome + 5.87 mm wall (converged boundary setting), plus a 5 mm wide metal strip through the full radome thickness above the wall, so the wall continues to the outer surface of the radome. 63.1 M cells.
Result
The first model (N8wr) ran the metal strip into the absorbing side boundaries. The field energy grew by about 2.5 dB/ns instead of decaying: the run is numerically unstable and gives no usable result. The corrected model (N8wr2) has the strip only over the length of the wall; it is now solving.
Why
On the radome + wall models the port data suggest that the remaining leak crosses the wall inside the radome slab. If so, cutting the radome above the wall could give 10 dB or more.
simulated  L2wa3

2×12 boundary check: −50.0 / −50.9 dB; full-panel estimate unchanged

Model
2×12 + 2×12, radome + wall, re-run with 3 mm air and 8 absorbing layers above the radome (before: 1.75 mm air, 4 layers).
Result
Corner / mid-gap channel: −50.0 / −50.9 dB, i.e. 0.70 / 0.89 dB worse than with the old setting (−50.7 / −51.8 dB). 18.3 / 14.9 dB better than the plate alone.
Meaning
Same size of correction as on the 4×4 model (about 1 dB). The full-panel estimate already included 1 dB, so it stays at about 65 dB.

Model

simulated estimate  L2w L2c

Full gap length (2×12): the wall stops the build-up along the gap; full panel estimated at about 65 dB

Model
TX 2×12 + RX 2×12 (the columns on each side of the gap, all 12 rows), 5 mm gap. Radome + wall: 86.5 M cells, 2.3 h. Plate only, no radome: 52.8 M cells, 2.1 h.
Result
Plate: −31.7 / −36.1 dB (corner / mid-gap). Radome + wall: −50.7 / −51.8 dB (old boundary setting; corrected on 25 Oct to −50.0 / −50.9). Going from 4 to 12 rows makes the coupling only 2.5 / 0.7 dB worse with the wall, against 13.5 / 9.0 dB with the plate.
Estimate
Full-size 5 mm plate result + improvement of the wall on the 2×12 model + correction for the six TX columns not in the 2×12 model + 1 dB boundary correction: B about −45 dB (−49.5 to −40.8) → leakage about −36 dBm → isolation about 65 dB (61–69), about 5 dB short.
Caveats
Estimate, not a full-size simulation. One channel type per model; generic radome.
simulated  A1cg8d72h18

Full size, 8 mm gap, plate 1.8 mm high: 53.8 dB

Model
TX 8×12 + RX 8×12, 8 mm gap, plate with 11 grooves 0.72 mm deep, 1.8 mm high, no radome; RX corner channel. 190.6 M cells, 11.8 h, peak memory 65.8 GB. Made from the built 8 mm model by replacing only the plate (6 min instead of a 4.75 h rebuild).
Result
±20°: B = −34.0 dB at 99.9 GHz → −24.8 dBm → 53.8 dB (16.2 dB short). ±10°: 55.9 dB. Boresight: 62.2 dB. 8.6 dB better than the 5 mm / 1.4 mm full-size run.
Caveats
Gap and plate changed together, so the 8.6 dB cannot be split between them. 0.72 mm grooves were used because the run started before the 0.78 mm / 1.8 mm result was in (that one was 0.5–1.8 dB better on the 4×4 model). The full system was 10.4 dB worse than the 4×4 model with this plate.
simulated  N8w N8wg8 N8a

Radome + metal wall up to the radome: the biggest single improvement

Model
4×4 + 4×4 with a generic radome (flat slab, εr 3.0, loss tangent about 0.01, 0.888 mm, 5 mm above the patches) and the plate raised to a 5.87 mm wall touching the radome. 5 mm gap (55.2 M cells, 1.5 h) and 8 mm gap (58.0 M cells, 1.5 h). The older radome-only run was re-evaluated on the same grid.
Result
Radome alone: −28.0 dB, 5.6 dB worse than no radome. Radome + wall, 5 mm: −49.0 / −50.2 dB as run (−48.2 / −49.1 after the boundary correction): 21 dB better than the radome alone and about 9 dB better than the plate. 8 mm with the wall: −48.3 / −49.7 dB, no better than 5 mm.
Meaning
Closing the gap up to the radome is the strongest measure found. With the wall, a wider gap no longer helps. The port data suggest that what is left crosses the wall inside the radome slab, which led to the radome-cut run.
Caveats
4×4 model; generic radome; the 8 mm wall also had different grooves (11 × 0.72 mm).
simulated  N3cg8d72 N3cg8h18 N3cg8d72h18

Plate height matters, groove depth hardly does (8 mm gap)

Model
4×4 + 4×4, 8 mm gap, 11-groove plate. Changed one at a time from 0.78 mm deep / 1.4 mm high: depth 0.72 mm, height 1.8 mm, both. 34–38 M cells, 1.5–2.6 h each.
Result
Reference −40.7 / −42.3 dB. Depth 0.72: −42.5 / −41.1 (mixed, about the noise level). Height 1.8: −45.6 / −46.3 (4.9 / 4.0 dB better). Both: −45.0 / −44.4.
Meaning
A taller plate, which closes more of the gap above the patches, is what helps. This pointed to raising it all the way to the radome.

23 Oct 2026

simulated  N3c_d072t3

Shallower grooves (0.72 mm) do not move the plate's best frequency into the band

Model
4×4 + 4×4, 5 mm gap, plate as before but 0.72 mm deep grooves. 32.5 M cells, 1.5 h.
Result
−39.9 / −38.1 dB, 0.4 / 2.6 dB worse than 0.78 mm. The plate's best frequency stays at 90.7 GHz instead of moving to the predicted 96–98 GHz.
Meaning
The strong effect at 90–91 GHz is the plate removing the open gap's own coupling peak, which sits where the wavelength equals the 3.3 mm element pitch (90.8 GHz). It is set by the array, not by the groove depth, so the unit-cell prediction (22 Oct) did not hold. 0.78 mm was kept.
assessment

Frequency-domain solver with tetrahedral mesh: probably not faster here

What
A benchmark was prepared and its result conversion tested against the time-domain data (identical results). From the time-domain results, a broadband frequency sweep would need 8–24 frequency samples in 95–100 GHz with the plate.
Meaning
The time-domain solver gives the whole 85–105 GHz band per RX channel in one 1.5–3.5 h run of the 4×4 model. The frequency-domain solver would need a full solve per sample on a fine mesh. Not benchmarked; low priority.

simulated  A1c1

Full size, 5 mm gap, plate 1.4 mm high: 45.2 dB; worst case caused by a grating lobe

Model
TX 8×12 + RX 8×12, 5 mm gap, 7-groove plate 0.78 mm deep, 1.4 mm high, no radome; RX corner channel. 171.7 M cells, 18.1 h, peak memory 63.4 GB.
Result
±20°: B = −25.4 dB at 99.9 GHz (TX at az −16°, el +6°) → −16.2 dBm → 45.2 dB (24.8 dB short). ±10°: 48.6 dB. Boresight: 59.3 dB. Median direction: 63.4 dB; 19 % of directions meet −41 dBm. The full system is 15.3 dB worse than the 4×4 model with the same plate.
Analysis
Steering away from the RX array is 7.4 dB worse than towards it. At 99.9 GHz the wavelength (3.00 mm) is below the 3.3 mm pitch; in the worst directions a grating lobe runs along the panel surface towards the RX corner. The strongest TX elements are 23–33 mm along the gap, not the nearest one: energy guided along the gap adds up almost in phase. A pitch below 2.89 mm would keep that lobe out of the ±20° scan.

simulated  unit cell (eigenmode)

Unit-cell screening of groove depth (later not confirmed on the array)

Model
One period of the corrugation (0.7 mm period, 0.4 mm groove, 1.4 mm high), CST eigenmode solver, depth 0.62–0.80 mm; 18 s per solve.
Result
Scaled to the 4×4 result, the prediction was that 0.714 mm deep grooves would move the plate's best frequency from 90–92 GHz to 97.5 GHz.
Outcome
Tested on the array on 23 Oct: it did not hold, because the 90–92 GHz feature comes from the array pitch, not from the grooves.

21 Oct 2026

simulated  N3c

First test of the corrugated plate (4×4, 5 mm gap): about 7 dB

Model
4×4 + 4×4, 5 mm gap, no radome, metal plate 1.4 mm high with 7 grooves 0.4 × 0.78 mm along the gap. 33.2 M cells, 3.5 h.
Result
−33.7 / −33.5 dB without the plate → −40.3 / −40.7 dB with it (6.6 / 7.2 dB better). The improvement is largest at 90–91 GHz (25–30 dB), below the band; in band it is 1–9 dB depending on frequency.
Ringing
With the plate the field energy decays slowly (about 2.4 dB/ns instead of 14.5 dB/ns) and is still at −49 dB after 4 ns. Later plate runs were stopped at 3 ns; this changes the worst case by about 0.2 dB.
