Rev D mechanics review

The rev D session is redesigning the module box. The card's SSMC edge now faces the rear, the card is turned to stand at the right wall, and every output goes to a three-row rear panel. These sheets show the points raised in this session's review of the rev D design notes (draft 3). An independent agent re-checked each finding before its figure was drawn to scale from the rev D geometry code.

Status

The rev D session adopted most of these points in its draft 3.1 and built its own 3D model. Since then you have moved on to rev E, with only RF outputs on the front panel and everything else at the rear, so rev D is superseded. These sheets are the record of what the review found. The rev E design study carries over the lessons: jack spacing, washer clearance, honest drive checks and realistic cable loss.

Rev D geometry: draft 3.1, 22 Oct 2026. Findings checked against draft 3 and the current geometry.

Sheet F1

Grommet holes in the rear access cover

Partly right

Draft 3 had the problem: nine Ø6 holes at the 7.34 mm SSMC pitch leave only 1.34 mm of metal between holes, where 3.2 to 4 mm is normal for a 2 mm plate. Most small grommets also overlap their neighbours, but the thin web is the real issue. The current geometry already uses one slot instead. That slot is closed at the bottom, so cables cannot go in sideways.

Change

1) Spec §10: delete the "nine Ø6 holes with split grommets" text. §16 c already overrides it, and the 3D-model builder must not model the holes.

2) Spec §16 c: drop "brush-strip edge trim (cables in sideways)". The slot is closed by a 2.0 mm bridge, so cables cannot go in from the side, and I found no catalog brush strip for a 6 mm slot. Pick one of these instead:

(a) A bare, deburred, chamfered 6 mm slot. Each ADC cable is threaded plug-first: the 4.57 mm hex passes with 0.71 mm per side.

(b) Edge trim on the long edges only, fitted after threading, or a slot about 7.2 mm wide (estimate). Trim on a 6 mm slot leaves about 3.7 mm clear, which is less than the plug hex.

(c) If side entry is really needed, an open-ended slot, which means a two-piece cover.

3) geom coverHoleWeb:

- Raise webMin from 2.0 toward the 3.175 mm guide, or record why 2.0 is accepted.

- Add the captive-screw-to-cover-edge webs to the check: 2.0 at cs-1 and cs-2, 2.5 at cs-3 and cs-4.

- Enlarge the slot-bottom bridge. A rectangular cover gets at most 2.53. A local cover tab at x ≥ +68 down to y ≈ 22 would give about 5.2 and stay clear of the J18 washer (design estimate).

4) adcAccess text: change the window "y 25 … 166" to 25.5 … 166 to match geom.

Sheet F2

Cover edge vs the J18 lock washer: spec §10 at y 24 overlaps it; geom now at 25.2

Partly right

Spec draft 3 puts the rear access cover's bottom edge at y 24 mm. The J18 lock washer reaches 24.15, so the two would overlap by 0.15 mm, and both sit on the panel's outside face. The geometry has already moved the edge to 25.2 (1.05 mm clear) and now tests the washer, but §10 still says 24. The largest standard washer would leave 0.93 mm, just under the 1 mm rule.

Change

Spec §10 (revd_spec.md line 168 to 171): change the cover to "x +52…+84, y 25.2…183.5, 2 mm" and the window to "y 25.5…166", matching §16 d. Replace "Bottom edge flush (1.2 mm above the J18 nut)" with "1.05 mm above the J18 lock washer (Ø11.9); coverToNut tests the washer circle". Do not adopt the claim's 24.5 fix: it leaves 0.35 mm, which fails the existing 1 mm rule. The edge must be at least 25.15, or 25.27 for the largest washer ASME B18.21.1 allows. BOM: hold the row A lock-washer OD at 11.9 mm or less (Keystone 4691 is 11.91 nominal), or specify a Ø10.2 SMA lock ring (1.9 mm clear). If neither holds, raise the edge to 25.3 or more and raise the window with it. Code: add a check that the cover laps the window. The bottom lap is now only 0.3 mm, against the "5 mm land around the window" in §10. Alternatively, notch the cover around J18 or lower the window.

Sheet F3

Output drive levels with rev D lengths: both drive checks still FAIL

Partly right

Both output drive checks fail on the main page and still fail with the rev D lengths. The one-bit legs reach 0.58–1.15 V pk against the 3.1 V needed. The RF-DAC path is 5.4–9.0 dB short at 5 GHz. The shorter D0–D7 and the new W1–W8 pigtail change this by only about ±0.3 dB. Rev D's model has no drive check yet, so for now it shows CHECK and TO MEASURE.

Change

1) In spec §16a, which replaces §0.6, give the Standard-box, rear-output numbers instead of the four-option spans. With rev D lengths the one-bit drive is 0.58–1.15 V pk, 8.6–14.6 dB short, and needs 28.6 dB per leg. The RF-DAC drive is 5.8–8.8 dB short at D 370 + W 100, and 5.4–9.0 dB across all cases. Change "in findings" to "Analog frontend OUTPUT SWING card (fidelity.js 281–293) and overview requirements swing row (one-bit only; RF-DAC only in DAC-only mode)".

2) When rev D merges a3cf222's model.js, remove the chain() gate lens.out === 'front' (page_src model.js 942) and keep rev D's lens.w.dac / w.part.dac gate. Otherwise the W1–W8 loss disappears and the shortfall reads 0.5 dB too good (5.28–8.28 dB).

3) Pick the U11/U12 P1dB spec from rev D's rear outputs, not from whether a W cable is present. State that the need is 20.02–20.23 dBm, above the 20 dBm rear spec (build.js 345 "P1dB ≥ 20 dBm"), so raise that line or mark it FAIL.

4) Follow §16b: put LINKS.wcoax back on the 1.13 mm fit and add a separate 0.047 in link type. Cite the hand-formable EZ-FLEX.47 or EZ-FLEX.47-CU-DS, not EZ-Microflex 47, which is a flexible cable with a 7.4 mm minimum bend.

5) Reconcile the W1–W8 part length: 100 mm in geom vs "≈ 120 mm" in §7. At 120 mm the shortfall is 0.06–0.31 dB larger.

The drive FAILs themselves remain open user decisions; nothing in rev D fixes them.
