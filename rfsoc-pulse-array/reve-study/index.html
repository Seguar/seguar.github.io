Rev E carrier study

Round 2 of the layout study for the next carrier. As you asked, both families, upright and flat, now extend the carrier so the RF-DAC cells sit right at the card's DAC jacks. Dense PCB SMA rows are tried against panel-nut jacks, and the box is priced as in-house sheet metal. Round 1's best upright (V2) and flat (V5) layouts stay as reference columns. Nothing is built yet: pick a layout and it goes onto the main page.

Recommendation

Build rev E as U-b: an upright card whose 8 RF-DAC cells sit on a small vertical RF board right beside the card's DAC jacks (D0-D7 cut from 450 to 80 mm), with the one-bit cells next to J1 on the carrier. It is the cheapest layout that meets the specs ($4,915 per 4-module array with two PAs on the one-bit chain) and performs best. The best flat layout, F2, costs the same ($4,928) and is 2U smaller in a 4-module rack, but it needs a stepped front panel, the unproven straddle connector and passes the one-bit check by only 0.12 dB.

3D models of all six variants →

Iteration 2 extended the carrier in both families so the RF-DAC cells sit at the card's DAC SSMC jacks, kept the one-bit cells near J1, tried dense PCB SMA rows, and priced the box as in-house sheet metal. The three area results were combined with their checkers' corrections on one basis (synth2.py).

Upright wins. In the upright box the card's SSMC column is vertical. U-b stands a small RF board (VB) on the front panel beside that column. This cuts D0-D7 to 49-60 mm (80 mm cable part, against 230 mm in V2 and 450 mm today) and gives the best RF-DAC margin, +5.32 dB. The one-bit cells stay on the carrier top face beside J1: lanes up to 107 mm, no vias. U-a (one carrier, no VB) cannot get D0-D7 below a 160 mm part, because the DAC jacks sit 74-126 mm above the carrier.

Flat is close but second. In the flat box the ADC and J57 straight plugs lie in the carrier plane, so the RF-DAC arm must start 31 mm in front of the card and the front ends 87 mm ahead of it. D0-D7 drop to a 130 mm part, but the one-bit lanes grow unless the one-bit jacks move onto a recessed bay. F2 (4 + 4 one-bit jacks on a bay 58 mm back) is the only flat layout that passes with pick A, by 0.12 dB.

Cost. Cost does not separate the layouts: the five that meet the specs cost $4,915-4,953 per 4-module array (within 0.8 %), with the sync unit now $772.5. U-b is the cheapest. It is also the cheapest if the balun measures about 1.5 dB and pick A suffices: $4,763.

Rack. U-b fits 3 per 5U shelf: 13U for 4 modules (11U with a 2 mm height trim) and 18U for 8. The flat layouts take 11U and 19U.

Open items for U-b:

  • A route for the J35 / J64 cables, which collide with D6 / D7.
  • A clamp for the VB jacks.
  • A head-orientation rule for the jumpers.

The layouts

U-a

U-a dense (upright, extended two-face carrier, no VB)

Viable

The carrier is extended so the RF-DAC cells (top face) sit beside the card plane and the one-bit cells (bottom face, R5) sit near J1. The front is dense: 8 + 8 jacks at a 12.9 mm pitch, with the bottom row set back 7.5 mm. D0-D7 cannot become short, because the DAC jacks are 74-126 mm above the carrier. They are 178-194 mm with a 210 mm cable part, only 20 mm shorter than V2. Moving the carrier front forward does not help either: the part stays at 210 mm and every one-bit lane grows by 36 mm. Box 152 x 188.5 x 275 mm, 2 per 5U shelf, $4,953 per 4-module array with pair B. Other front option: panel nuts at 15.2 mm (U-a nuts: box 180 mm wide, D part 230 mm, $4,945).

For

  • No vertical board, no jumpers between boards, no J35 / J64 conflict: the simplest upright
  • Short one-bit lanes (80-101 mm): pick A +0.91 dB WARN, pair B +2.27 dB PASS
  • Low-risk vertical J1
  • RF-DAC +4.68 dB PASS

Against

  • D0-D7 part 210 mm: misses the 'as close as possible' goal (floor 160 mm for any U-a)
  • The 7.5 mm set-back row cannot be a bent joggle (1.25 mm of room): it needs a window in the panel and a separate recessed strip
  • One-bit cells in a 16.5 mm pocket under the carrier (vents); 1 via pair per lane with the SMT J1
  • 2 per shelf: 13U for 4, 23U for 8 modules
U-b

U-b dense (upright card + vertical RF board beside the SSMC column)

Recommended

The 8 RF-DAC cells sit on a small vertical RF board (VB, 65 x 106 mm, single-sided) that stands on the front panel beside the card's SSMC column. Short SSMC jumpers (49-60 mm, 80 mm cable part, 0.39 dB) replace today's 450 mm D cables. The carrier is single-sided, with the 8 one-bit cells on its top face next to J1: lanes 85-107 mm, no vias. The box is 125 x 174.5 x 275 mm, 3 per 5U shelf. Parts cost $1,036 per module and $4,915 per 4-module array with pair B on the one-bit chain, the lowest of all layouts. Other front option: panel nuts at 15.2 mm (U-b nuts: 149 mm wide, 2 per shelf, $4,939).

For

  • Shortest D0-D7 (80 mm part) and best RF-DAC margin (+5.32 dB)
  • One-bit lanes <= 107 mm on the top face, no vias: pair B +2.13 dB PASS (pick A +0.77 WARN, +2.47 dB if the balun measures about 1.5 dB)
  • Cheapest layout that meets the specs: $4,915 per 4-module array ($8,954 for 8)
  • Low-risk vertical J1; two single-sided boards on one panel
  • Smallest box and footprint; 3 per shelf: 13U / 18U (11U / 15U with the 2 mm trim)

Against

  • J35 / J64 jumpers (W21 / W22) collide with the rising D6 / D7 jumpers and have no route yet; heads toward the solder side need an exception to the brief's head rule
  • The VB jacks have no clamp in the item (breaks R1): add right-hand standoffs and a comb bar (about +6 mm width and +$2 per module, estimate)
  • The VB-side jumper heads must turn toward the card heads (RG-178 bend radius 10.2 mm); otherwise the box grows by 15 mm (still 3 per shelf)
  • Needs pair B on the one-bit chain unless the balun measures about 1.5 dB
  • About 12 W of RF-DAC cells on the VB in the intake path (estimate): gap pad to the panel
F1

F1 dense bay (flat, all cells on the top face, one-bit row on a bay)

Not recommended

The card lies flat beside a split carrier: an RF-DAC board on an arm 31-87 mm in front of the DAC jacks (D0-D7 53-92 mm, 130 mm part) and a main carrier with J1. A flush front puts the one-bit inputs 105-136 mm ahead of the TX pads: lanes reach 170 mm and even pair B only WARNs (+0.62 dB). This column therefore moves the one-bit row onto a bay 55 mm back. Lanes are then 52.5-130 mm and pair B passes at +1.58 dB. Box 291.7 x 64.6 x 331 mm, 2U. $5,021 per 4-module array, the most expensive. Other front options: panel nuts on the bay (322.5 mm wide, pair B +1.18 dB, $5,029) and a flush front (fails the spec).

For

  • Lowest box (64.6 mm), 2U per module, 11U for 4 modules
  • D0-D7 part 130 mm, RF-DAC +5.07 dB
  • All cells on the top face, no pocket

Against

  • Needs pair B and still has the lowest passing one-bit margin of the iteration-2 layouts (+1.58 dB)
  • Most expensive ($5,021 per array) and dominated by F2
  • Stepped front panel; straddle J1 (medium risk)
  • 8 lane vias; split boards need 2 assembly orders
F2

F2 = 2R dense bay (flat, one-bit 4 top + 4 bottom on a bay)

Viable

This is the best flat layout and the only one that passes the one-bit check with pick A. The RF-DAC arm and D0-D7 are as in F1 (130 mm part, +5.07 dB). The one-bit jacks sit on a bay 58 mm behind the RF-DAC nose, 4 on the top face and 4 on the bottom face, with the bottom row set back 7.5 mm. With the checker's TX swap (odd lanes to the bottom cells), the lanes are 47-92.5 mm with 4 vias, and pick A gives +1.12 dB (+1.09 dB with the via). Box 291.7 x 78.1 x 331 mm, 2U; $4,928 per 4-module array, $13 more than U-b. The pure F2 (8 one-bit on the bottom face) reaches only pick A +0.22 / pair B +1.58 dB in a bay, and pair B +0.62 dB WARN flush. Other front option: panel nuts on the bay (2R nuts bay, 308 mm wide, pick A +1.03 dB, $4,930).

For

  • Passes with pick A (no pair B), with the shortest one-bit lanes of all (<= 92.5 mm)
  • D0-D7 part 130 mm, RF-DAC +5.07 dB
  • 11U for 4 modules (2U each), stacks on the bench
  • Cost within $13 of U-b

Against

  • Pick A passes by only 0.12 dB; it needs the TX swap and a bay at least 56.5 mm deep; with minimum data-sheet gain it WARNs
  • Straddle J1 (medium risk); card swap needs a 10 mm wider QSFP cut-out and standoffs fixed to the card
  • Stepped front, socket or hand only; 3 lids; two-sided; 4 one-bit cells in a 21.5 mm pocket
  • fig_f2_top shows the unswapped lanes with the vias inside the bottom cells; the vias must sit behind the bottom row
  • 19U for 8 modules (U-b 18U)
V2

V2 std-centre (iteration 1 reference)

Not recommended

Iteration-1 upright fallback. Re-costed with the iteration-2 prices (KWE13X26 + panel nut, in-house box, sync unit $772.5): $4,938 per 4-module array with pair B. Its RF-DAC margin is +4.58 dB at its 230 mm part; iteration 1 showed +4.39 dB, which the AFE check had computed at 270 mm. U-b beats it on D0-D7 (80 against 230 mm), one-bit margin (+2.13 against +1.90 dB with pair B), rack (18U against 23U for 8 modules) and cost ($23 less per array).

For

  • Known, checked layout; vertical J1

Against

  • D0-D7 230 mm; superseded by U-b and U-a

V5 flat-1face (iteration 1 reference)

Not recommended

Iteration-1 pick. Re-costed with the iteration-2 prices and an in-house 415 x 68 x 275 mm box: $4,930 per 4-module array with pair B (iteration 1: $4,864 with pick A, a stock box, Molex jacks and a $528 sync unit). Dense jacks, stack-up, analog chain, cost

DENSE

Dense SMA rules (HiTech-style front)

Yes, the jacks can sit as tightly as on the HiTech FMC_SMA_LVDS board, but only for plugs tightened by hand or with a slotted socket driver. The HiTech grid is about 10.9 x 9.9 mm (estimated from photos; no drawing is published), so its plug nuts almost touch.

Minimum pitch for one row:

  • hand-tight: 10.5 mm;
  • slotted socket torque driver: 11.5 mm (the cable must leave sideways within 25-30 mm);
  • open-end torque wrench: 14.3 / 14.8 / 15.2 mm (handle at 0 / +-15 / +-30 deg). Use 15.2 mm, the same as the panel-nut layouts.

Two faces (R5). With plain right-angle THT jacks the rows are 14.4 mm apart. Aligned columns at one depth collide through the board, because both jacks need the same leg and pin holes. Either offset the columns by at least 7.55 mm (pitch >= 15.1 mm), or set the bottom-face jack back by at least 7.5 mm. Only the 14 mm-thread KWE13X26 allows the set-back.

In the iteration-2 layouts the AFE cell pitch (12.86-12.9 mm) sets the dense pitch, not the jack. Dense rows run at 12.9 mm with a 3.7 mm plug-nut gap, socket or hand only. Panel-nut rows run at 15.2 mm (nut gap 6.0 mm, washer 3.3 mm) and take an open-end wrench. Dense fronts make U-b 24 mm narrower (3 per shelf instead of 2).

Cost. The saving comes from the jack, not the density:

  • KWE13X26 + comb bar: $0.85 per jack (+ panel nut: $0.75), against $4.91 for the Molex bulkhead;
  • about $83 less per module (20 jacks).

Dense fronts drop R1's panel nut and the 5.5 mm nut-gap rule in favour of a comb clamp bar. That is a user decision.

STACK

4-layer FR-4 stack-up and lane link points

Unchanged from iteration 1: JLC's free JLC04161H-7628 4-layer stack on standard FR-4.

  • 100 ohm lane pair on L4: w 0.225 / s 0.200 mm.
  • 50 ohm coplanar line: w 0.304 / gap 0.200 mm.
  • Lane loss: 0.0220 / 0.0401 dB/mm at 5 / 10 GHz; worst case 0.0262 dB/mm at 5 GHz.

Longest lanes now:

  • F2: 92.5 mm (2.03 / 3.71 dB);
  • U-a: 101 mm;
  • U-b: 107 mm;
  • F1: 130 mm;
  • iteration 1: V2 117 mm, V5 147 mm.

Via counts. The costed vertical J1 is SMT on the carrier's top face:

  • U-a's bottom-face cells need one through-via pair per lane;
  • U-b's top-face cells need none.
  • On the straddle J1 the TX tails are on the lower row: F1 (top cells) needs 8 vias, F2 needs 4.

A via pair costs at most 0.06 dB at 10 GHz (about 0.03 dB at 5 GHz). Plain FR-4 stays a deliberate deviation from R4: a mid-loss laminate would save about 0.8 dB on the 107 mm U-b lane (0.0149 against 0.0220 dB/mm).

What you decide

Your decisions

  • Approve U-b dense (upright card + vertical RF board) as rev E. Or choose F2 (flat, 2R dense bay) if 2U per module in a rack, or one main board without a vertical board, matters more than the margins and the vertical J1.

  • Allow the J35 / J64 (W21 / W22) plug heads in U-b to point toward the card's solder side, an exception to the brief's 'heads toward the component side' rule, so they clear the D6 / D7 jumpers.

  • Front jacks:

    • Dense: KWE13X26 + comb clamp bar, no panel nut, socket or hand only. U-b is 125 mm wide, 3 per shelf.
    • Panel nuts at 15.2 mm (R1): an open-end wrench works on the top row. U-b nuts is 149 mm wide, 2 per shelf, +$24 per array.

  • Measure the UWB2-50E balun loss at 5 GHz:

    • about 1.5 dB: pick A everywhere (U-b $4,763 per array);
    • about 3 dB: pair B on the one-bit chain (+$152 per array).

  • ADC cable exit: a slot in the front panel next to the RF jacks (all layouts), against R1's RF-only front panel.

  • Box: in-house sheet metal (estimated $57-72 including shop time), or a stock Bud CU-881 ($90; 2 per 7U shelf, so 17U for 4 modules).

  • Try the U-b 4U trim (lid gap 2.5 mm, standoff 7.1 mm): 11U for 4 modules and 15U for 8.

  • Jumpers: quote flexible 0.047 in / RG-178-class SSMC right-angle jumpers. U-b needs 8 of 80-90 mm plus W21-W23.

  • Sync unit: LMK04832EVM + adapter (about $772 per array; one unit for up to 8 modules).

  • Keep plain FR-4 (R4 deviation from iteration 1): a mid-loss laminate would save about 0.8 dB on the 107 mm U-b lane.

Risks

  • Cost does not decide. The five layouts that meet the specs are within $38 per 4-module array (0.8 %). The estimated lines (jumpers $15-40 each, lids, retention, box shop time) are larger than that, so U-b and F2 are a cost tie. Performance, rack size and connector risk are the robust differences.
  • U-b open items: - The J35 / J64 jumpers (W21 / W22) collide with the rising D6 / D7 jumpers and need a route. Heads toward the solder side would be an exception to the brief's head rule. - The VB jacks need a clamp, because the item had neither panel nuts nor a comb bar on them. - The VB-side jumper heads must turn toward the card heads, because the RG-178 bend radius is 10.2 mm. Otherwise the box grows to about 140-146 mm wide (still 3 per shelf). - The KE8-G jack drawing has not been read, so the 3 mm VB-to-panel gap and the exposed thread are unverified.
  • One-bit drive margin is the tightest spec. Every iteration-2 layout except F2 needs pair B if the balun loses about 3 dB at 5 GHz. F2's pick-A pass (+1.12 dB, +1.09 with its via) depends on the TX swap and on a bay at least 56.5 mm deep. With minimum data-sheet gain every layout WARNs.
  • Dense fronts drop brief R1 (panel nut + lock washer) and the 5.5 mm nut-gap rule in favour of a comb clamp bar. No open-end wrench fits below 15.2 mm, and the slotted socket driver needs a flexible cable leaving sideways. Hand-tight mating is not torque-controlled.
  • KH-SMA-KWE13X26 is rated DC-6 GHz with VSWR 1.5. Measure it on the carrier footprint before committing. Its $0.5999 LCSC price could not be re-read; at the iteration-1 $0.7773 a module costs $3.6 more.
  • Flat layouts: - The straddle J1 fit is still unproven (10.5 mm coplanar gap, tail positions, 1.57 mm board). - A card swap needs a QSFP cut-out 10 mm wider and corner standoffs fixed to the card. - The stepped bay panel takes socket or hand tools only. - fig_f2_top draws the vias inside the bottom cells, with L4 crossings. The swap plus vias behind the bottom row fix this.
  • The in-house box price is an estimate ($57-72 including $25 of shop time). The stock comparison boxes do not fit the flat layouts cleanly: the Hammond 1444 needs a sub-panel, and the 19 in 2U chassis is 1 mm too shallow.
  • Rack numbers: - The U-b 4U trim leaves 0.2 mm (lid gap 2.5 mm, standoff 7.1 mm). - U-b nuts at 3 per shelf (W 148) breaks the 1 mm washer clearance unless the VB moves in by 1 mm.
  • The upright D cables pass 2.84 mm (centre line) from the ADC7 straight-plug zone at the DAC0 head exit. The card geometry sets this, in every upright layout.
  • U-a's 7.5 mm set-back row cannot be bent as a joggle in one sheet. It needs a window in the panel plus a separate recessed strip on spacers.
  • Not yet checked in iteration 2: - the rd6 part-to-part collision pass; - the W21-W23 and J65 ribbon routes in the flat layouts; - the PDN; - thermal CFD (about 12 W on the U-b VB; pair B adds 8 PAs); - the standoff and screw positions taken from V2.
  • Prices were seen on 2026-10-22/23 at qty 10-100, without shipping, VAT or tariffs. The sync EVM is the DigiKey price; the TI store price was not readable.
