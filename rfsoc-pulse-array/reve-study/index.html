Rev E carrier study

You asked for a front panel with only the 16 RF outputs on on-board SMA jacks, everything else at the rear, a barrel jack for the kit's 12 V brick, a cheap 4-layer FR-4 carrier, and a fresh look at a wide flat layout. This study compares five layouts against those requirements, with costs, and recommends one per setup. Nothing is built yet: pick a layout and it goes onto the main page.

Recommendation

Build rev E as V5 (card flat beside a wide carrier, all 16 jacks in one row on the top face): it is the cheapest layout that meets the drive spec ($4,864 per 4-module array, or $5,016 with the two-PA one-bit chain) and the densest in a rack (11U for 4 modules); keep V2 (upright card centred, +2 %) as the fallback if the straddle edge connector does not fit.

The checked layout, stack-up, AFE and cost results were combined into one price and one scorecard per layout. The five cheap layouts cost within 3 % of each other: about $4,860-4,980 per 4-module array, including the sync unit. V5 (card flat, one row of 16 jacks on the top face) is the cheapest layout that passes the 3 V / 5 GHz drive check, and the densest: 11U for 4 modules, against 13-17U upright. Its weak points are a 147 mm longest one-bit lane (two PAs per one-bit channel if the balun loses 3 dB) and an unproven straddle connector. V2 (upright, card centred) is the lower-risk fallback at +2 %. V1, V3 and V4 are not recommended.

Study run 22-23 Oct 2026; prices seen 22-23 Oct 2026 (list prices, qty as stated).

The five layouts

V1

V1 std-wall (upright card near the left wall)

Not recommended

The upright card sits near the left wall with its SSMC edge at the front. The 8 one-bit jacks sit under and the 8 RF-DAC jacks over a 160 mm carrier (pitch 20 mm, stagger 10 mm) in a 170 x 189 x 275 mm box. With a through-hole J1 the lanes cannot escape at the wall, so the card moves to x -55 (lanes 92-165 mm). With the stocked SMT J1 it can stay at x -74 (lanes 46-182 mm). Either way V1 has the longest upright lanes, and it saves only $14 per array over V2.

For

  • Cheapest upright layout ($4,962 per 4-module array, $1,109 per module)
  • Smallest box (170 mm wide); 2 per 5U shelf in a custom box
  • Short D0-D7 (270 mm, 1.33 dB at 5 GHz)
  • Stocked vertical Gen5 SMT J1 at $2.63
  • Card fan has 98 mm of free air

Against

  • Longest upright lanes: 165 mm, 3.64 dB at 5 GHz and 6.63 dB at 10 GHz
  • One-bit margin -0.62 dB on the corrected basis (FAIL); only +0.74 dB (WARN) even with pair B
  • Front at the limit: nut gap 5.52 mm against 5.5, bottom-row wrench handle 0.4 mm per side
  • One-bit cells in a closed 17 mm pocket under the carrier
  • 13U (custom box) or 17U (stock Bud) for 4 modules
V2

V2 std-centre (upright card centred over the carrier)

Viable

As V1, but the card stands over the middle of the carrier (x +5), held by a rear angle and a front L-bracket. It has the shortest two-face lanes (69-117 mm) and the shortest D0-D7 (230 mm). It costs $4,976 per 4-module array (+2 % over V5), uses the cheap stocked vertical J1 and has 0.7 dB more one-bit margin than V5. In a rack it takes 13U (custom box) or 17U (stock Bud CU-881) for 4 modules, and its 170 mm front edge passes the nut rule with only 0.02 mm to spare. This is the fallback if the straddle connector of V5 fails.

For

  • Best one-bit margin of the cheap layouts: +2.24 dB (item), +0.54 dB (corrected), +1.90 dB with pair B
  • Shortest D0-D7: 230 mm, 1.13 dB at 5 GHz
  • Low-risk vertical Gen5 SMT J1 ($2.63, 865 in stock)
  • Small bench footprint per module; the stock Bud box leaves about 18 mm above the card

Against

  • +$112 per array over V5
  • Front gaps at the limit (nut gap 5.52 mm, 0.4 mm wrench handle per side); V2w fixes this for +$4 per array
  • Two-sided assembly; one-bit cells on the bottom face (rework needs the carrier out)
  • Rear fan must move to the card's component side (x about +55)
  • Front L-bracket clears the J57 plug by only 1-3 mm; the D-cable drop passes 3-4 mm from the ADC straight-plug space
  • Front panel must be <= 2.5 mm thick (2 mm), not today's 3 mm
V2w

V2w (tuned V2 in a 200 mm wide box)

Viable

This is V2 in a 200 mm wide box, with a jack pitch of 24 mm and a 12 mm stagger. It fixes the front edge: nut gap 6.94 mm, wrench margin 2.54 mm, bottom-row handle 2.4 mm per side. It still fits 2 boxes per 450 mm shelf and the Bud CU-881. The cost is lanes up to 129 mm and +$4 per array.

For

  • Comfortable torque-wrench and nut-driver access on both rows
  • Same rack density as V2
  • Only +$4 per array over V2

Against

  • Longest lane 129 mm (2.84 dB at 5 GHz); one-bit margin +0.24 dB corrected (+1.60 dB with pair B)
  • +19 % carrier area (511 cm2)
  • All other V2 cons
V3

V3 std-wide-1face (upright, 16 jacks in one top row)

Not recommended

The card stands upright in a 262 mm wide box, with all 16 jacks in one top-face row (pitch 16 mm) and single-sided SMT. V3 has the best upright front edge, the best thermal path and the shortest lanes (108 mm). Against that: one box per rack shelf (23U for 4 modules), a 683 cm2 carrier above JLC's 650 cm2 large-size step, the longest upright D0-D7 (330 mm), and no stock box under $100. The result is $6,293 per array, +29 %.

For

  • Shortest one-bit lanes (108 mm, 2.37 dB at 5 GHz) and the best one-bit margin (+2.46 dB item, +0.75 dB corrected, +2.11 dB with pair B)
  • Single-sided cells, one lid, no lane vias
  • Comfortable front (nut gap 6.80 mm, wrench 2.40 mm) and a 14 mm lid gap

Against

  • 23U for 4 modules, 44U for 8 (one box per shelf)
  • Carrier 683 cm2 pays JLC large-size fees ($57.46 per assembly order plus a PCB adder)
  • No stock box under $100 found; the 19 in 5U chassis costs $426
  • D0-D7 330 mm (1.62 dB)
  • Most expensive: $6,293 per 4-module array
V4

V4 flat-2face (card flat, jacks on two faces)

Not recommended

The card lies flat beside a 183 mm carrier on a straddle edge connector, with 8 + 8 jacks on two faces at a 22 mm pitch, in a box about 79 mm tall (2U per module). It is the second cheapest ($4,879 per array). But the one-bit cells are spread over 154 mm of edge, which gives the longest lanes: 196 mm, 4.30 dB at 5 GHz and 7.84 dB at 10 GHz. The one-bit drive FAILS on the corrected basis (-1.35 dB) and reaches only +0.01 dB even with pair B.

For

  • 2U per module: 11U for 4 modules, 20U for 8
  • Low-cost stock chassis ($64)
  • Good front gaps (nut 6.22 mm, wrench 1.82 mm)

Against

  • Longest one-bit lanes (196 mm): fails the drive check
  • Two-sided assembly; the bottom-face cells sit in a 17 mm still-air pocket, and rework needs the carrier out
  • Card fan inlet only about 9.4 mm in the stock 76 mm chassis (12 mm wanted)
  • Straddle connector risk (coplanar fit, drawing, board thickness)
V5

V5 flat-1face (card flat, 16 jacks in one top row)

Recommended

The card lies flat beside a wide carrier on a Gen5 straddle edge connector. All 16 jacks are in one top-face row at a 15 mm pitch, and the one-bit cells are clustered next to J1. The box is low: about 415 x 68 x 275 mm as laid out, or the stock Hammond 1444-17123 chassis (432 x 305 x 76 mm). V5 is the cheapest layout ($4,864 per 4-module array, $1,084 per module) and the densest in a rack (2U per module). It needs a confirmed straddle drawing, and two PAs per one-bit channel if the balun loses 3 dB at 5 GHz.

For

  • Lowest cost: $4,864 per array with pick A, $5,016 with pair B on the one-bit chain
  • Densest: 11U for 4 modules, 20U for 8; stacks on the bench
  • All 16 cells on the top face: one lid, rework from the top, no closed hot pocket
  • Front passes with margin (nut gap 5.80 mm, washer 3.10 mm, wrench 1.40 mm)
  • Simple card retention: 4 corner standoffs + a rest post

Against

  • Lanes up to 147 mm (3.23 dB at 5 GHz, 5.88 dB at 10 GHz): one-bit margin +1.53 dB on the item basis but -0.17 dB corrected; pair B on the one-bit chain (+$152 per array) gives +1.19 dB
  • Straddle connector not proven: only the x16 Amphenol 10163106-A41410HLF is confirmed as a stocked Gen5 straddle part; the coplanar fit (gap 10.5-12 mm), the tab clearances and the 1.57 mm board thickness need the drawing
  • D0-D7 410 mm (2.01 dB); the RF-DAC chain still has +3.7 dB
  • In the stock 305 mm deep chassis the carrier grows to about 241 x 300 mm (723 cm2, JLC large-size fees, included), and the card I/O sits about 35 mm inside the rear wall (estimate)
  • Two-sided assembly anyway (straddle tails on both faces), or hand-solder the bottom row

Stack-up, analog chain, cost

STACK

4-layer FR-4 stack-up and lane link points

Use JLC's free 4-layer stack JLC04161H-7628 on plain FR-4. It has 0.21 mm of 7628 prepreg under both outer layers and solid ground on L2 and L3. Widths: - 100 ohm lane pair on L4: w 0.225 / s 0.200 mm. - 50 ohm coplanar line: w 0.304 / gap 0.200 mm. With +-10 % on every input at once, the impedance stays within 87-113 / 43-56 ohm, so the cheap tolerance class is fine. Lane loss on standard FR-4 is 0.0055 / 0.0141 / 0.0220 / 0.0401 dB/mm at 1 / 3 / 5 / 10 GHz. The Df 0.020 behind these points is a 1 GHz figure, so use the worst case (0.0262 dB/mm at 5 GHz) for drive checks. A true mid-loss laminate would save at most 1.4 dB (V4), less than the spread between layouts. Plain FR-4 is therefore a deliberate deviation from R4 for the user to confirm. The cheapest J1 is SMT, so lanes to cells on the opposite face need one through-via pair each. That via adds no stub and costs at most 0.06 dB at 10 GHz.

AFE

AFE line-up, amplifier pick and drive checks

Pick A uses the same parts in both chains: a GVA-84+ driver, a 150 ohm || 0.9 pF tilt equaliser and a PHA-83W+ PA, for $9.47 per channel in amplifiers. The one-bit chain puts the balun first, and the RF-DAC chain has no U10. RF-DAC drive PASSES in every layout, at +3.7 to +4.4 dB at 5 GHz. One-bit drive depends on the lane length: - **AFE item's basis** (balun 1.5 dB): V1, V2, V2w, V3 and V5 PASS; V4 WARNs. - **Check's corrections** (balun about 3 dB at its 5 GHz band edge, 3.0 V at the jack): only V2, V2w and V3 stay positive (WARN); V1, V4 and V5 FAIL. - **Pair B** (2 x PHA-83W+) on the one-bit chain adds 1.36 dB for $152 per array, and then V2, V2w, V3 and V5 PASS. With data-sheet minimum gain, every layout WARNs. AFE main-line parts cost $418 per module at qty 100 ($428 at array quantity), against $997 today.

COST

Price table highlights and the cost model

JLC 4-layer FR-4 costs about $0.0123 per cm2 per board plus $25 per order. Above 650 cm2 a large-size PCB adder and a $57.46 assembly fee per order apply. Two-sided assembly adds only $33.77 per order. The bare PCB is the smallest line, at $11-16 per module. The big lines per module are: - AFE parts: $428. - Jumpers: about $300 (estimate). - Connectors: $232-235. - Box: $64-426. The five cheap layouts land within 3 % of each other, and the boxes decide their order: the flat layouts fit a $64 low aluminium chassis, the upright ones need a $90 steel cabinet, and V3 has no stock box below $426. The largest unpriced line is the 11 SSMC jumpers per module. The same 11 are in every layout, so they cannot change the ranking.

SYNC

Sync unit cost line (evaluation board)

The sync unit is a TI LMK04832EVM ($523.95) plus a Raspberry Pi Pico ($4): $527.95 per array of 2-7 modules, the same in every layout. One module needs none. For 8 modules, 16 outputs are needed (CLK + TRIG for each module) and the EVM has 14, so the 8-module costs add a second EVM ($523.95); a fan-out buffer would also work but is not priced. The EVAL-HMC7044 costs $1,038.40, twice as much. The LMK04828BEVM price could not be reproduced: the TI page says 'Not available on TI.com', and a Mouser Israel search result showed $641.90. It saves nothing, so keep the LMK04832EVM.

What you decide

Your decisions

  • Approve V5 (flat, one row of 16 jacks) as rev E, or choose V2 (upright, card centred, +2 %) if the straddle-connector risk is not acceptable.
  • Get and check the straddle connector drawing (Amphenol 10163106-A41410HLF, or a confirmed x8 part): card-to-carrier gap 10.5-12 mm, tab clearances, 1.57 mm board thickness (specify the finished thickness at JLC).
  • Measure the UWB2-50E loss at 5 GHz (S2P or a test board). About 1.5 dB keeps pick A; about 3 dB calls for pair B on the one-bit chain (+$152 per array).
  • Define '3 V pk': the waveform peak, or the amplitude of a 5 GHz tone (a 1100 pattern gives up to +2.1 dB more fundamental).
  • Balun-first one-bit, or single-ended one-bit without the balun (-$433 per array, about 1.5 dB less below 4 GHz, no common-mode rejection).
  • Accept plain FR-4 instead of R4's mid-loss default (it would save at most 1.4 dB).
  • Box: the stock Hammond 1444-17123 (carrier 723 cm2, I/O recessed about 35 mm), or a custom 275 mm deep box (carrier 648 cm2, about $7 per carrier less in PCB and assembly fees; box not quoted).
  • Jumpers: quote custom 1.13 mm micro-coax D0-D7 and W21-W23 (rated to >= 5 GHz) against catalogue assemblies.
  • Bulkhead SMA: Molex 73251-2200 ($4.91), or LCSC SMA-KWHD ($1.10, saves $305 per array) once its drawing shows >= 9.5 mm of thread past the clamp face and a 4.6 mm axis.
  • Shielding on the first prototype: fence vias only (saves $50-100 per array), or folded lids.
  • Impedance control +-10 % ($34 per order) on the prototype, or the free +-20 % test.
  • Rear jacks in a box with fixed walls: a removable rear plate, or recessed sync jacks.
  • 8 modules: a second LMK04832EVM ($524), or a fan-out buffer.
  • Measure: the torque-wrench handle width and nut-driver OD, the HamGeek brick plug (5.5 x 2.5 mm most likely) and the PHA-83W+ gain from 1 to 50 MHz.

Risks

  • The cost gaps between V1, V2, V2w, V4 and V5 are only 0-3 % ($4,864-4,980 per array). The estimated lines (jumpers $15-40 each, lids $5-20, retention parts) are bigger than these gaps, so the cost order among these five can flip. The robust differences are the rack size (11U against 13-17U) and the one-bit lane length.
  • The jumpers are the biggest unpriced line: 11 per module at $15-40 (page estimate), i.e. $660-1,760 per array. Catalogue SSMC-SSMC assemblies ($101-168 each) would add about $3,200 per array, and some are rated only to 3 GHz.
  • The one-bit drive margin is thin. On the corrected basis (balun about 3 dB at 5 GHz, 3.0 V at the jack), V5 with pick A is at -0.17 dB and needs pair B on the one-bit chain. With data-sheet minimum gain every layout WARNs, and the GVA-84+ loses about 0.6 dB more at 85 C (not in the margins).
  • PA input peaks on NRZ edges: with the equaliser after the driver, 50-70 ps edges can reach 21-22 dBm sine-equivalent, above the PHA-83W+ +18 dBm CW rating. Moving the RC equaliser ahead of the GVA-84+ costs nothing; otherwise check with the S2P files.
  • Straddle edge connector (V4, V5): no straddle part has been checked against its drawing. The flat item's layout overlaps the card and carrier by 0.5 mm (the gap must be 10.5-12 mm), and the only confirmed stocked Gen5 straddle is an x16 part. The 1.57 mm board thickness must be held against JLC's +-10 %.
  • Stock boxes: a carrier with bulkhead jacks at both ends cannot be lowered into a box whose front and rear walls are both fixed (check the chosen box; the Hammond 1444 is a folded chassis). This needs a removable rear plate or recessed rear sync jacks. The stock flat chassis is 30 mm deeper than the layout, so the V5 carrier reaches 723 cm2 and the card I/O sits about 35 mm inside the rear wall (estimate). In the 76 mm chassis, V4's fan inlet is only about 9.4 mm.
  • Front jack access: every layout passes the 5.5 mm nut rule but would warn on the main page's 8 mm finger / wrench check. V1 and V2 pass with only 0.02 mm to spare. The tool sizes are estimates; measure them.
  • Panel thickness: the jack thread allows at most a 2.5 mm panel. Today's 3 mm upright front panel and the 3.2 mm 19 in chassis panels fail without a spot-face.
  • FR-4 loss inputs: Df 0.020 is a 1 GHz figure and the roughness Rq 1 um is an assumption. The worst case is +19 % at 5 GHz, i.e. 0.6 dB more on a 147 mm lane.
  • Lane-to-lane far-end crosstalk is about -19 to -26 dB at 5 GHz at a 3w gap over 100-210 mm (uncalibrated estimate). Keep the lanes short, and use a 4w gap where it fits.
  • Thermal: 25-30 W of AFE heat per module plus the card. Each PHA-83W+ runs about +41 C over its tab and needs thermal vias. The two-face layouts have a closed 17 mm pocket under the carrier.
  • The 1 MHz low end is unverified: the PHA-83W+ is specified only from 50 MHz, and the one-bit chain needs about 14 uH per choke position.
  • Not yet checked: the rd6 3D collision pass for the chosen layout; the W21-W23 and J65 ribbon re-routing; the ADC / GNSS straight-plug keep-out against the D-cable drop (upright); and the PDN at about 55-65 W.
  • Prices were seen on 2026-10-22/23 at qty 10-100, without shipping, VAT or tariffs (Mouser shows 10-32 % tariff notes). Stock changes daily (Amphenol x16 straddle 455 pcs, Rego straddle 18 pcs).
