Concept

Overview: how the signal gets to the DUT

Patterns go from the PC into each RFSoC card. The card generates the outputs in two ways: an RF-DAC for arbitrary waveforms and a GTY serializer for fast one-bit pulses. The carrier board amplifies both to 3 V, sets each channel's DC level, and drives the DUT. One clock source keeps all four modules on the same clock: a sync unit, or the master module through a passive splitter. A small PC, a Minisforum MS-A2 on the Ethernet switch, runs the system.

System: four modules, one clock source, one PC

Every module gets the clock and the trigger from one source: a sync unit, or the master module through passive splitters (SYSREF is made on each card, or sent through a second splitter), or a daisy chain. The control PC, a Minisforum MS-A2 on the Ethernet switch, only uploads patterns, sets DC levels and reads back status.

Four design rules carry over to layout: put the eight one-bit (GTY) strips nearest the PCIe slot so the 20 Gb/s lanes stay short, and the eight RF-DAC strips beyond them, since their signals arrive by SSMC jumpers anyway; route all eight one-bit lanes as length-matched 100 Ω pairs; keep each output channel as a strip that runs straight to its front-panel SMA; and give the DC-level buffers their own ground return so DC current stays out of the RF ground under the amplifiers.

Pulse fidelity: lowest frequency and edge speed

Which parts set the lowest frequency and the edge speed, and what does a 500 ps, 3 V pulse look like after the chain? Every coupling capacitor, balun and bias choke adds a high-pass corner. The source edge, the amplifier and the balun set the high end.

What the low corner does to a pulse

A high-pass corner fL makes the top of a pulse sag. Over a pulse of width τ the top drops by 1 − exp(−2π fL τ). For 500 ps even a 1 MHz corner costs only 0.3 %, so short pulses pass AC coupling well. The low corner matters for long pulses, slow DC-level steps and long patterns.

After AC coupling, a unipolar pulse train settles so that its average is zero. The baseline moves down by duty × amplitude: 500 ps every 100 ns at 3 V gives 15 mV. Corners well below 1 / period keep this shift. Corners near or above it let the baseline recover between pulses.

What sets the edge

The 10–90 % rise time is about 0.35 / bandwidth. The GTY edge (about 20 ps), the RF-DAC (about 60 ps), the amplifier and the balun each limit the bandwidth. Their rise times add roughly as root-sum-square.

The carrier parts are on the Carrier schematic: T1, L1/L2/L5 and C1–C4 on the one-bit outputs, C11 and L10–L12 on the DAC outputs. The card's own DAC path is in Card findings.

Formulas used here

  • Capacitor into 50 Ω: f = 1 / (2π · 50 Ω · C). Choke across 25 Ω: f = 25 Ω / (2π L).
  • Droop over a pulse of width τ: 1 − exp(−2π fL τ). For several corners, use the sum of the corners.
  • Cascade −3 dB: solve |H(f)|² = ½ with |H|² = Π f² / (f² + fi²) for the high-pass corners and Π 1 / (1 + (f / fi)²) for the low-pass limits.
  • Rise time 10–90 % ≈ 0.35 / BW. Parts combine roughly as root-sum-square.
  • Baseline shift after AC coupling = duty × amplitude (0.5 % × 3 V = 15 mV).
  • P1dB for a Vpeak pulse into 50 Ω: 10·log10(V² / (2 · 50 Ω) / 1 mW). 3 V needs 19.5 dBm, 4 V needs 22 dBm.

Step 5

Sync and timing

All modules have to start their patterns on the same clock edge. The trick is that the trigger only arms each module. The actual start is the next SYSREF edge, which comes from one source and reaches every card with the same delay. So the trigger can arrive anywhere inside a 200 ns window and every module still starts on the same cycle.

Trigger alignment

TRIG reaches each module a few ns apart because cables and receivers differ. With SYSREF gating the trigger only arms the module and all modules start on the next SYSREF edge, while starting straight on TRIG copies the arrival spread into the outputs, rounded to the 4 ns fabric clock.

Card J32 (SYS_CLK_OUT): can it be a reference?

J32 is the vertical SMA on the card's top edge (schematic page 18), and the card's only external clock output.

  • Source. LMK04828 SDCLKOUT5. It shares its clock group with DCLKOUT4 (ADC_REFCLK1, an RF-ADC tile we do not use), so J32 is free. It can be a device clock, VCO ÷ 1 to 32 (≥ 93.75 MHz at VCO1 = 3000 MHz; 250 MHz = ÷ 12), or the SYSREF divider output, VCO ÷ 8 to 8191 (5 MHz = ÷ 600).
  • Electrical. Single-ended, DC-coupled LVPECL: P side through R651 33 Ω with a 240 Ω emitter pull-down (R338), N side to GND through C395 0.1 µF. About 0.4–0.6 Vpp into 50 Ω (estimate). Needs an inline DC block.
  • Card plan at VCO1 = 3000 MHz: PL_CLK 250 MHz = ÷ 12, DAC_REFCLK 500 MHz = ÷ 6, SYSREF 5 MHz = ÷ 600.

Yes, as the GTY reference (recommended)

J32 at 250 MHz → short coax W23 → carrier J45 "GTY REF IN" → C20 DC block → U27 single-ended-to-LVDS buffer (LMK1D1204 class; check that it takes a single-ended input) → PCIE_CLK edge pins (10 nF series on the card) → GTY quad A refclk1, shared with quad B. QPLL0 N = 40 → VCO 10 GHz → 20 Gb/s full rate.

The GTY clock is then locked and phase-fixed to the card's own PL_CLK, DAC and SYSREF clocks. 250 MHz = 50 × 5 MHz has one fixed phase to SYSREF; 156.25 MHz had 4. Jitter is about 100 fs from the LMK plus tens of fs from the buffer, which is fine. Carrier U21 becomes an assembly option: fallback and master clock.

Start-up rule. The card LMK locks before the GTY QPLL is released.

As a SYSREF source or a chain clock: two special cases

Master + 2 splitters. The master card's J32 runs the SYSREF divider at 5 MHz → DC block → splitter B → every SYSREF IN. J32 is then busy on the master, so its GTY reference falls back to carrier U21 (156.25 MHz, one of 4 phases against SYSREF). The other modules keep their own J32 loop.

Card-only chain. J32 at 5 MHz → DC block → next card J66, derived SYSREF, no carriers. The GTY then runs from the card’s free-running SI5341 (one-bit rows not locked to the DACs). Bring-up only. One output cannot carry both CLK and SYSREF, so with the R699 SYSREF mod a chain still needs the carrier relay (chain B1). See the Daisy-chain option.

Start-up sequence

  1. The root locks: the sync unit locks its LMK04832 to the OCXO, or the master carrier locks U21 to Y1 and sends 5 MHz out of J29.
  2. On each carrier, U20 and U22 (AC-coupled input buffers) take CLK IN and SYSREF IN and pass them to the card jumpers. U21 is fitted only on a master.
  3. On each card, the LMK04828 locks PLL1 to CLK on J66 (CLKin0, jumper J67 on 2-3). SYSREF on J35 reaches its SYNC pin through the R699 wire mod (see Card findings); the LMK re-clocks it with a per-card delay trim. With the derived SYSREF (one 5 MHz clock) the card makes SYSREF itself. The dividers sync over SPI, and J32 starts its 250 MHz.
  4. Card J32 reaches the GTY over W23, carrier J45, the DC block and U27, into PCIE_CLK. Rule: hold the GTY QPLL0 in reset until the card LMK reports PLL1 and PLL2 lock, then release it (N = 40, 10 GHz).
  5. Each card runs RF-DAC multi-tile sync and reports the measured latency to the PC.
  6. The PC sets one common target latency for all 32 DACs, so every card pads to the slowest.
  7. GTY lanes come up with TX buffer bypass and phase alignment; the PL loads the patterns.
  8. TRIG: the PC arms the re-timer in the sync unit, or the master FPGA drives its TRIG IO (pin A10 through card J64) while every other FPGA listens on its own. Every module starts on the same SYSREF edge after TRIG.
  9. Apply the stored skew table (bit shifts and interpolator steps), or measure a fresh one.

If the card LMK loses lock, hold the GTY in reset and repeat from step 3.

Splitter levels (estimate)

In the master options a passive 1:N RF power splitter feeds every CLK IN (and SYSREF IN with two splitters). The master's J29 CLK/SYSREF OUT is a 3.3 V LVCMOS driver, series-terminated into 50 Ω: about +8 dBm. A transformer splitter loses about 10·log10(N) + 1 dB.

That is too little for an LVCMOS-threshold input, so U20 (CLK IN) and U22 (SYSREF IN) become AC-coupled differential-input buffers (LMK1D class with a single-ended input bias, check). They need about 0.2 Vpp, which leaves ≈ 8 dB of margin at 8 modules. Their outputs stay as before, and U22 keeps one LVCMOS output for card J35.

Pick a transformer-type splitter that covers ≥ 1 MHz (Mini-Circuits ZFSC-n-1 class, check), terminate unused ports in 50 Ω, and use the same cable length to every input, the master's loop-back included. The splitter passes the square edges and U20 restores full swing, so card J66 still sees a fast edge (check on the bench).

Card J57 and J64: can we use them?

Both are SSMC jacks in the card's front-edge column (schematic page 17), reachable with the inlet plate off. Neither needs a board change.

J64 PPS_IN: yes, as TRIG IO

J64 → R245 33 Ω series → FPGA pin A10 (3.3 V HD bank), with R248 1 kΩ to ground and a TPD4E001 ESD diode. It is a direct FPGA I/O, so the FPGA sets the direction.

  • As input: a direct trigger into the PL, LVCMOS33 threshold, 1 kΩ load. The source should be series-terminated.
  • As output: the FPGA driver plus 33 Ω is close to a 50 Ω series-terminated source into coax.
  • No tweak. Do not add a 50 Ω shunt: it would load the driver.

So the rear TRIG IO SMA (J28) is cabled straight to card J64: one internal coax, W25, ≈ 480 mm as routed at the 235 mm card (≈ 400–560 mm for 160–320 mm cards, estimate), from the rear panel to the card's front edge (column position i = 1). The master drives, the others listen. This removes the carrier TRIG receiver U23, the PERST# trigger route and J42; J65 IO3 becomes spare.

Several modules: a passive resistive star from the master's TRIG IO (into high-impedance CMOS inputs it keeps ≈ full swing; the arm resistors damp reflections), or a short multi-drop bus along a chain. TRIG still only has to land inside one 200 ns SYSREF period.

J57 GNSS: yes, for time and arming, not as a clock

J57 → NEO-M8N RF_IN, with antenna bias from VCC_RF through L40 10 nH, so it takes an active antenna. The receiver's TIMEPULSE goes only to FPGA pin A9 (GNSS_PPS), not to the LMK. GNSS gives time of day and a PPS of tens-of-ns class (NEO-M8N timepulse accuracy: check the datasheet). That is not a reference for ps alignment.

  • Time stamps: absolute time on every run.
  • GNSS-armed start: every module arms on the same GNSS second and starts on the first common SYSREF edge after it. The PPS spread (tens of ns) is far below the 200 ns SYSREF period, so no TRIG cable is needed when all modules see GNSS: one active antenna and a 1:N GNSS splitter with DC pass on one port, or one antenna per module with sky view. The modules still share CLK. Each module reports where the PPS fell in its SYSREF period; if one lands within ≈ 50 ns of an edge, the PC moves to the next second.
  • Set-ups far apart: common time without a cable between them.

Daisy-chain option

The sync unit is not the only way to share a clock. One module can be the master: it holds the reference oscillator and drives the trigger, and the others take both from it, through a passive splitter or along a chain of short cables. Every carrier is the same board with a master strap.

Master + splitter · recommended

One external splitter. The master's J29 CLK/SYSREF OUT sends 5 MHz into a passive 1:N RF splitter that feeds every CLK IN, its own included. TRIG IO goes through a resistive star. Derived SYSREF, no box, no card mod.

Master + 2 splitters

True SYSREF without a box. Splitter A carries CLK, splitter B carries SYSREF from the master card's J32 to every SYSREF IN. Needs the R699 mod on every card.

Daisy chain

Hop by hop. J29 of one module feeds CLK IN of the next; TRIG IO runs as a short bus. Fewest cables, but delay and drift add up per hop.

Sync unit (star)

A separate 1U box sends CLK, SYSREF and TRIG to every module over phase-matched cables. It is described in Sync & timing.

One clock does both jobs

The chain and the one-splitter star carry a single 5 MHz clock, the SYSREF rate. Each module locks its two LMK04828s to it in nested zero-delay mode, with the SYSREF divider as the feedback. The divider then has one fixed phase to the incoming edge, so every module makes its own SYSREF. No SYSREF cable runs between modules, and no module samples another module's SYSREF.

Modules are numbered from the master: module k sits k hops after module 0. The GTY reference does not ride on the chain: each card loops its own J32 (250 MHz, one phase to the 5 MHz edge) back through carrier J45, so carrier-relayed chains keep J32 free. Only the card-only chain uses J32 for the chain clock. This stage uses 5 MHz to match Sync & timing.

Recommendation

  • Use the derived SYSREF unless a true SYSREF is needed. It removes the SYSREF cable, every sampling window between modules and the LMK04832 output limit. It needs only LMK settings, proven on one card first.
  • 2 to 8 modules in one rack: Master + splitter. No box, 1U and about 10 W saved, one passive splitter and a resistive TRIG star, 2N + 1 timing cables (N equal-length CLK), identical carriers with a master strap. Every module sits one cable from the root, so no drift builds up.
  • True SYSREF wanted: Master + 2 splitters, or the sync unit. Both need the R699 mod on every card; the splitters avoid the box.
  • 2 to 4 modules side by side: chain B1 is the alternative with the fewest cables. With 8 modules seven hops drift about 52 ps at ΔT = 5 °C with α = 1.5 ps/°C per hop (to measure), so use a star.
  • Card-only chain: for bring-up of two bare cards, or for DAC-only modules. It uses up J32, so the GTY runs from the card’s free-running SI5341 (one-bit rows not locked to the DACs); with carriers fitted, use carrier U21 or a carrier-relayed chain instead.
  • Design every carrier master-capable. The extra parts are small (Y1, U21, JP1, JP3), and the same board then works with a sync unit, in a chain and in either master star.

Corrections this study found

  1. As built, J66 goes through jumper J67 (pin 3) to CLKin0, and J35 goes through R345 to CLKin1. A separate SYSREF would have to use CLKin0 and push CLK onto J35, which shows no connector on the vendor photos.
  2. With the R699 wire mod (remove R345 and R699, wire J35 to the LMK SYNC pin) J35 becomes a SYSREF input on the SYNC pin, re-clocked by the LMK. CLK then goes into J66 with J67 on 2-3: carrier J32 → card J66, carrier J33 → card J35.
  3. So with the mod buffered and re-clocked SYSREF variants (chain B2, the sync-unit star) become possible per card, each with a SYSREF delay trim. The derived-SYSREF chain needs no mod and still feeds J66 only.
  4. J66 has a 49.9 Ω DC shunt (R344), so a carrier output into it needs a series 0.1 µF DC block. The SYNC pin is a CMOS input: drive J35 with DC-coupled LVCMOS, series-terminated at the driver.
  5. Card J32 is a DC-coupled LVPECL output (SDCLKOUT5), so a J32 → J66 link needs an inline SMA DC block. J32 is the card's only clock output: in the card-only chain it runs the SYSREF divider at 5 MHz and can no longer be the GTY reference, and one output cannot carry both REF and SYSREF, so with the R699 SYSREF mod a chain still needs the carrier relay (B1).
  6. CLK must be a square wave at card J66: CLKin needs at least 0.15 V/ns (aim for 0.5 V/ns), and a 1 Vpp 10 MHz sine gives only 0.031 V/ns. The carrier U20 buffer squares up whatever arrives on CLK IN, a splitter output included.

What builds up along a chain

Delay and drift. Each hop adds a buffer and a cable, about 3 ns: 1.44 ns of 300 mm coax plus 1.50 ns of LMK1C1104. With eight modules the last one runs 21 ns behind the master. That delay is fixed, so calibration removes it with a start offset in 4 ns steps plus a fine trim. Drift is different. Every buffer in the path drifts with temperature, and the last module sees the sum: k·α·ΔT, 22.5 ps at four modules and 52.5 ps at eight.

Jitter does not build up. Each buffer adds about 20 fs. The PLL1 loop of the next LMK04828 filters it out. Every module's output jitter is set by its own LMK04828 and DAC PLL, about 100 fs, whether it sits first or eighth.

Trigger. The trigger only has to land inside one 200 ns SYSREF period. The master drives its TRIG IO 100 ns after a SYSREF edge. On the passive TRIG IO bus the trigger sees only cable delay; the simulator still counts one buffer per hop, the conservative case. Either way it stays near the middle of the window on every module, at 100 ns ± 5 ns.

Re-clocking per hop. U21 can sample SYSREF on its 400 ps VCO grid and send it on with 50 ps trims. That adds a sampling point at every hop. An edge within about 60 ps of a VCO edge can slip one VCO cycle between power-ups, so every hop needs a trim. An untrimmed point lands that close 30 % of the time, so with seven hops one needs a trim 92 % of the time. The derived SYSREF needs none.

Failure. A chain depends on every module before it. If a module loses power, the modules after it lose the reference. Running the relay buffers from an always-on rail, or fitting a bypass relay, keeps the chain alive.

Card only. The HamGeek card can chain on its own: J32 of one card, in SYSREF-divider mode at 5 MHz, into J66 of the next through a DC block. That keeps the DAC outputs aligned. It uses up J32, which otherwise loops back through the carrier as the GTY reference. The GTY then runs from the card’s free-running SI5341 (one-bit rows not locked to the DACs); with carriers fitted, use carrier U21 or a carrier-relayed chain instead.

Which one. Master + splitter is the recommended option: no box, no card mod, one passive splitter and a resistive TRIG star, and every module sits one equal cable from the root, so nothing builds up with N. The chain needs the fewest cables, 2(N − 1) short ones, but for eight modules the drift over seven hops uses most of the 50 ps budget. Both save the sync unit, 1U of rack and about 10 W. When a true SYSREF is wanted, use Master + 2 splitters or the sync unit.

12 V: a star, never a chain

Each module draws 4.55 A at 12 V. Chained through eight modules the first link would carry 36.4 A, and with 0.3 m links of 12 AWG the last module would see about 1.16 V less (contacts to measure). Feed every J30 from a distribution bar or a bus bar on the base plate: 1 m of 16 AWG drops only 0.12 V.

Ethernet

Keep the external switch with N + 1 ports (option E1): a dead module never cuts the others off. Option E2 puts a 3-port GbE switch on every carrier (U25, J37 to J39). It saves the switch and the star of patch cables, costs about 1 W per carrier (to measure) and adds about 12 µs per hop, which does not matter for control. The two RS-485 ports on each card can carry a slow backup link along the chain.

    Mechanical concept

    The carrier lies flat in a small aluminium box and the HamGeek card stands upright in its slot, like a card in a PC, with its heatsink and fan facing the RF strips. The card's column of 19 SSMC connectors faces the front, about 31 mm behind the panel: room for the connector barrels, right-angle SSMC plugs and the bend of the 8 DAC jumpers back to the strips. Its I/O edge (Ethernet, DisplayPort, USB, both QSFP cages) lines up with a cut-out in the rear panel. The front panel carries the 16 RF outputs, so the cables to the DUT stay short and tidy, and in front of the card a removable inlet plate with the air slots. The plate carries no connectors: unscrew its four thumb screws and lift it off for direct access to the card's SSMC column (ADC inputs, GNSS, CLK0_IN). The rear panel takes power, Ethernet and the other card I/O, and the four sync SMAs: CLK IN, SYSREF IN, TRIG IO and CLK/SYSREF OUT. Inside, the 8 one-bit strips sit next to the slot, so the GTY lanes stay short; the 8 DAC jumpers loop back along the card and cross over them to the RF-DAC strips on the far side.     Card J32 as a reference

    Card J32 (SYS_CLK_OUT) is the card's only external clock output: LMK04828 SDCLKOUT5, single-ended and DC-coupled LVPECL (about 0.4–0.6 Vpp into 50 Ω, estimate), so every cable from it needs an inline DC block. Its clock group shares only DCLKOUT4 (ADC_REFCLK1, an RF-ADC tile we do not use), so J32 is free to run at VCO/1…32 (250 MHz = ÷12 at VCO1 3000 MHz) or from the SYSREF divider (5 MHz = ÷600).

    What stays the same

    The carrier design, the firmware image and the start-up sequence. Every module is identical, so a spare module can replace any other after a skew re-calibration.

    What changes

    The number of sync outputs: the LMK04832 has 14 outputs, enough for 4 modules (4 CLK + 4 SYSREF) with spares. 8 modules need 16 outputs, so add buffers. Cable sets, supply size and rack space grow linearly.

    Mixing with the existing ZCU216

    The ZCU216's CLK104 can take CLK and SYSREF from the same sync unit, adding 16 DAC channels to the array. It needs its own front end (an RFMC mezzanine), and its outputs will differ slightly from the HamGeek channels, so calibrate it as a separate group.

    DAC-only modules

    If a module only needs RF-DAC outputs, leave the one-bit front ends unpopulated and skip the GTY rework. The 16-DAC option then costs two cards plus two lighter carriers.

    Roadmap

    Roadmap and open items

    Five phases take the design from one card on the bench to a calibrated array at the DUT. Each phase ends at a gate, and each open item belongs to the phase that has to close it.

    1. P0

      Evaluate one card

      2–4 weeks

      Goal. Prove that one HamGeek card does what the design assumes before we draw the carrier.

      Tasks

      • Bring up one HamGeek card: power, JTAG, PCIe and the RF-DAC tiles.
      • Lock the LMK04828 PLL1 to an external CLK on J66 (CLKin0, J67 on 2-3).
      • Test card J64 as TRIG IO in both directions: drive FPGA pin A10 into 50 Ω coax and check the edge, then take a series-terminated trigger in and check the A10 threshold.
      • Optional: fit an active antenna on J57, read GNSS time and check the PPS on FPGA pin A9.
      • Do the J35 → SYNC wire mod on one card (remove R345 and R699, wire J35 to LMK_SYNC) and verify re-clocked SYSREF + MTS repeatability over power cycles.
      • Program J32 to 250 MHz and lock the GTY QPLL from it through the carrier buffer; confirm the GTY-to-SYSREF phase repeats over power cycles.
      • Run GTY TX0–3 at 20 Gb/s raw (QPLL0 N = 40) from that 250 MHz refclk on the PCIE_CLK pins.
      • Do the TX4–7 rework (fit C632–35 and C1022–25, remove the QSFP2 0 Ω parts) and repeat the 20 Gb/s test.
      • Measure the card DAC path (0.1 µF, TCM2-63WX+, SSMC) from 100 kHz to 6 GHz.
      • Decide between the UWB2-50E balun swap and the single-ended bypass.

      Open items

      Exit gate. Eight GTY lanes run at 20 Gb/s, the DAC path is measured, and the balun choice is written down. PLL1 locks to CLK on J66, J64 works as TRIG IO both ways, and on the modded card the MTS latency repeats over power cycles with SYSREF on J35.
