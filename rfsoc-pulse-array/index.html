Concept

Overview: how the signal gets to the DUT

Patterns go from the PC into each RFSoC card. The card generates the outputs in two ways: an RF-DAC for arbitrary waveforms and a GTY serializer for fast one-bit pulses. The carrier board amplifies both to 3 V, sets each channel's DC level, and drives the DUT. One sync unit keeps all four modules on the same clock.

Concept

System: four modules, one sync unit, one PC

Every module gets the reference, SYSREF and trigger over its own phase-matched cable from the sync unit, so no module depends on another. The PC only uploads patterns, sets DC levels and reads back status.

This is option 1, with a separate sync unit. Option 2 drops the box and daisy-chains the modules: see Daisy-chain option. Your choice there carries through power, rack height and the 3D model.

Three design rules carry over to layout: route all eight one-bit lanes as length-matched 100 Ω pairs; keep each output channel as a strip that runs straight to its front-panel SMA; and give the DC-level buffers their own ground return so DC current stays out of the RF ground under the amplifiers.

Pulse fidelity: lowest frequency and edge speed

Which parts set the lowest frequency and the edge speed, and what does a 500 ps, 3 V pulse look like after the chain? Every coupling capacitor, balun and bias choke adds a high-pass corner. The source edge, the amplifier and the balun set the high end.

What the low corner does to a pulse

A high-pass corner fL makes the top of a pulse sag. Over a pulse of width τ the top drops by 1 − exp(−2π fL τ). For 500 ps even a 1 MHz corner costs only 0.3 %, so short pulses pass AC coupling well. The low corner matters for long pulses, slow DC-level steps and long patterns.

After AC coupling, a unipolar pulse train settles so that its average is zero. The baseline moves down by duty × amplitude: 500 ps every 100 ns at 3 V gives 15 mV. Corners well below 1 / period keep this shift. Corners near or above it let the baseline recover between pulses.

What sets the edge

The 10–90 % rise time is about 0.35 / bandwidth. The GTY edge (about 20 ps), the RF-DAC (about 60 ps), the amplifier and the balun each limit the bandwidth. Their rise times add roughly as root-sum-square.

The carrier parts are on the Carrier schematic: T1, L1/L2/L5 and C1–C4 on the one-bit outputs, C11 and L10–L12 on the DAC outputs. The card's own DAC path is in Card findings.

Formulas used here

  • Capacitor into 50 Ω: f = 1 / (2π · 50 Ω · C). Choke across 25 Ω: f = 25 Ω / (2π L).
  • Droop over a pulse of width τ: 1 − exp(−2π fL τ). For several corners, use the sum of the corners.
  • Cascade −3 dB: solve |H(f)|² = ½ with |H|² = Π f² / (f² + fi²) for the high-pass corners and Π 1 / (1 + (f / fi)²) for the low-pass limits.
  • Rise time 10–90 % ≈ 0.35 / BW. Parts combine roughly as root-sum-square.
  • Baseline shift after AC coupling = duty × amplitude (0.5 % × 3 V = 15 mV).
  • P1dB for a Vpeak pulse into 50 Ω: 10·log10(V² / (2 · 50 Ω) / 1 mW). 3 V needs 19.5 dBm, 4 V needs 22 dBm.

Step 5

Sync and timing

All modules have to start their patterns on the same clock edge. The trick is that the trigger only arms each module. The actual start is the next SYSREF edge, which comes from one source and reaches every card with the same delay. So the trigger can arrive anywhere inside a 200 ns window and every module still starts on the same cycle.

Trigger alignment

TRIG reaches each module a few ns apart because cables and receivers differ. With SYSREF gating the trigger only arms the module and all modules start on the next SYSREF edge, while starting straight on TRIG copies the arrival spread into the outputs, rounded to the 4 ns fabric clock.

Start-up sequence

  1. The sync unit locks its LMK04832 to the OCXO and starts SYSREF.
  2. On each carrier, U21 locks to REF and aligns to SYSREF. The GTY clock is now frequency-locked to the shared reference.
  3. On each card, the LMK04828 locks PLL1 to REF on J66 (CLKin0, jumper J67 on 2-3). SYSREF on J35 reaches its SYNC pin through the R699 wire mod (see Card findings); the LMK re-clocks it with a per-card delay trim. The dividers sync over SPI.
  4. Each card runs RF-DAC multi-tile sync and reports the measured latency to the PC.
  5. The PC sets one common target latency for all 32 DACs, so every card pads to the slowest.
  6. GTY lanes come up with TX buffer bypass and phase alignment; the PL loads the patterns.
  7. The PC arms the TRIG re-timer. Every module starts on the same SYSREF edge after TRIG.
  8. Apply the stored skew table (bit shifts and interpolator steps), or measure a fresh one.

Daisy-chain option

The daisy chain removes the sync unit. One module is the master. It holds the reference oscillator and launches the trigger. Every other module takes the reference and the trigger from its neighbour and passes a copy on.

Option 1

Sync unit (star). One box sends REF, SYSREF and TRIG to every module over phase-matched cables. It is described in Sync & timing.

Option 2 · this stage

Daisy chain. No box. The modules pass one clock and the trigger along a line of short cables, and every carrier is the same board with a master strap.

One clock does both jobs

The chain carries a single 5 MHz clock, the SYSREF rate. Each module locks its two LMK04828s to it in nested zero-delay mode, with the SYSREF divider as the feedback. The divider then has one fixed phase to the incoming edge, so every module makes its own SYSREF. No SYSREF cable runs between modules, and no module samples another module's SYSREF.

Modules are numbered from the master: module k sits k hops after module 0. A 1.25 MHz chain clock would also fix the phase of U21's 156.25 MHz GTY clock, which takes one of 4 values against a 5 MHz edge. This stage uses 5 MHz to match Sync & timing.

Recommendation

  • Use the derived SYSREF in every topology. It removes the SYSREF cable, every sampling window between modules and the LMK04832 output limit. It needs only LMK settings, proven on one card first.
  • 2 to 4 modules: chain B1. No box, 1U and about 10 W saved, 2(N − 1) short cables, identical carriers with a master strap.
  • 8 modules: a star. Keep the sync unit, or build the master star: the master carrier with two 1:8 fan-outs. Seven hops drift about 52 ps at ΔT = 5 °C with α = 1.5 ps/°C per hop (to measure).
  • Card-only chain: for bring-up of two bare cards, or for DAC-only modules. It cannot lock the one-bit outputs.
  • Design every carrier chain-capable. The extra parts are small, and the same board then works in a star, a chain and a master star.

Corrections this study found

  1. As built, J66 goes through jumper J67 (pin 3) to CLKin0, and J35 goes through R345 to CLKin1. A separate SYSREF would have to use CLKin0 and push REF onto J35, which shows no connector on the vendor photos.
  2. With the R699 wire mod (remove R345 and R699, wire J35 to the LMK SYNC pin) J35 becomes a SYSREF input on the SYNC pin, re-clocked by the LMK. REF then goes into J66 with J67 on 2-3: carrier J32 → card J66, carrier J33 → card J35.
  3. So with the mod buffered and re-clocked SYSREF variants (chain B2, the sync-unit star) become possible per card, each with a SYSREF delay trim. The derived-SYSREF chain needs no mod and still feeds J66 only.
  4. J66 has a 49.9 Ω DC shunt (R344), so a carrier output into it needs a series 0.1 µF DC block. The SYNC pin is a CMOS input: drive J35 with DC-coupled LVCMOS, series-terminated at the driver.
  5. Card J32 is a DC-coupled LVPECL output (SDCLKOUT5), so a J32 → J66 link needs an inline SMA DC block.
  6. REF must be a square wave: CLKin needs at least 0.15 V/ns (aim for 0.5 V/ns), and a 1 Vpp 10 MHz sine gives only 0.031 V/ns.

The Card findings stage carries these corrections and the SYSREF mod checklist.

What builds up along a chain

Delay and drift. Each hop adds a buffer and a cable, about 3 ns: 1.44 ns of 300 mm coax plus 1.50 ns of LMK1C1104. With eight modules the last one runs 21 ns behind the master. That delay is fixed, so calibration removes it with a start offset in 4 ns steps plus a fine trim. Drift is different. Every buffer in the path drifts with temperature, and the last module sees the sum: k·α·ΔT, 22.5 ps at four modules and 52.5 ps at eight.

Jitter does not build up. Each buffer adds about 20 fs. The PLL1 loop of the next LMK04828 filters it out. Every module's output jitter is set by its own LMK04828 and DAC PLL, about 100 fs, whether it sits first or eighth.

Trigger. The trigger only has to land inside one 200 ns SYSREF period. The master launches it 100 ns after a SYSREF edge. Trigger and reference pass the same kind of buffer at every hop, so the trigger stays near the middle of the window on every module, at 100 ns ± 5 ns.

Re-clocking per hop. U21 can sample SYSREF on its 400 ps VCO grid and send it on with 50 ps trims. That adds a sampling point at every hop. An edge within about 60 ps of a VCO edge can slip one VCO cycle between power-ups, so every hop needs a trim. An untrimmed point lands that close 30 % of the time, so with seven hops one needs a trim 92 % of the time. The derived SYSREF needs none.

Failure. A chain depends on every module before it. If a module loses power, the modules after it lose the reference. Running the relay buffers from an always-on rail, or fitting a bypass relay, keeps the chain alive.

Card only. The HamGeek card can chain on its own: J32 of one card into J66 of the next, through a DC block. That keeps the DAC outputs aligned. It cannot lock the one-bit outputs, because their GTY reference has to come from the carrier.

Which one. For two to four modules the chain is the simpler system: no box, short cables, identical carriers. For eight modules the drift over seven hops uses most of the 50 ps budget, so a star wins: the sync unit, or a master module with a 1:8 fan-out. The chain saves the sync unit, 1U of rack and about 10 W, and replaces 3N phase-matched cables with 2(N − 1) short ones: 6 instead of 12 at four modules.

12 V: a star, never a chain

Each module draws 4.55 A at 12 V. Chained through eight modules the first link would carry 36.4 A, and with 0.3 m links of 12 AWG the last module would see about 1.16 V less (contacts to measure). Feed every J30 from a distribution bar or a bus bar on the base plate: 1 m of 16 AWG drops only 0.12 V.

Ethernet

Keep the external switch with N + 1 ports (option E1): a dead module never cuts the others off. Option E2 puts a 3-port GbE switch on every carrier (U25, J37 to J39). It saves the switch and the star of patch cables, costs about 1 W per carrier (to measure) and adds about 12 µs per hop, which does not matter for control. The two RS-485 ports on each card can carry a slow backup link along the chain.

Open questions to check on hardware

  • Continuity on a delivered card: J66 → J67 pin 3 → CLKin0, J35 → R345 → CLKin1, the default J67 position, R344 and R348 fitted, and R699 on LMK_SYNC for the SYSREF mod.
  • Nested zero-delay with the SYSREF divider as feedback and a 5 MHz PFD on the card's existing PLL1 loop filter: lock, stability, lock time. Simulate in PLLatinum Sim first.
  • Phase repeatability over at least 20 power cycles: card PL_CLK and PL_SYSREF against the 5 MHz edge, and U21's 156.25 MHz (4 states at 5 MHz, 1 at 1.25 MHz).
  • Zero-delay input-to-output offset of U21 and U46, and its temperature coefficient.
  • Buffer drift α of the LMK1C1104 per hop, and the chain drift over warm-up and a 5 to 10 °C swing.
  • J32 through a DC block: amplitude and slew at J66.
  • MTS latency reports across power cycles in a chain, and whether a forced capture slip shows up in them.
  • Whether the HamGeek boot firmware programs U46, and whether our firmware can own its SPI completely.
  • A squaring circuit for a lab 10 MHz sine on the master, with CLKin slew ≥ 0.5 V/ns.

The hop cables run between neighbouring rear panels: 250 mm on the bench and about 450 mm between rack shelves (estimate). Mechanics shows the panels; Sync & timing has the star for comparison.

Multi-board signals

Only the trigger is timing-critical, and it travels on coax from the sync unit. Everything else between modules (ready flags, latency reports, the common target latency) goes over Ethernet through the PC, where a few milliseconds don't matter.

The trigger has to be valid before the SYSREF edge it should start on. The PL samples it at 250 MHz, so a 200 ns SYSREF period leaves a very wide margin. If a hardware ready line is ever needed, IO8/IO9 are free and can be wired as an open-drain bus through the sync unit.

    Build · Mechanics

    Mechanical concept

    The carrier lies flat in a small aluminium box and the HamGeek card stands upright in its slot, like a card in a PC, with its heatsink and fan facing the RF strips. The card's column of 19 SSMC connectors faces the front, about 31 mm behind the panel: room for the connector barrels, right-angle SSMC plugs and the bend of the 8 DAC jumpers back to the strips. Its I/O edge (Ethernet, DisplayPort, USB, both QSFP cages) lines up with a cut-out in the rear panel. The front panel carries the 16 RF outputs, so the cables to the DUT stay short and tidy, and in front of the card a removable inlet plate with the air slots. The plate carries no connectors: unscrew its four thumb screws and lift it off for direct access to the card's SSMC column (ADC inputs, GNSS, PPS, CLK0_IN). The rear panel takes power, Ethernet and the other card I/O, and the four sync SMAs. Inside, the 8 DAC jumpers go to the strips, carrier J32 (REF) feeds card J66 (CLK0_IN) and carrier J33 (SYSREF) feeds card J35. Air enters at the front and leaves through a rear fan, passing the card's heatsink on the way.

    The card size now comes from the vendor photos: about 235 × 148 mm (±3 %), measured against the x8 edge fingers, the QSFP cages and the RJ45. The other dimensions are estimates. Card J35 has no visible connector in the vendor photos. With the SYSREF mod (remove R345 and R699, wire the J35-side pad of R345 to the LMK-side pad of R699; fit an SMA on J35 if the footprint is empty) J35 drives the LMK SYNC pin, so carrier J33 sends SYSREF there while J32 sends REF to J66. Without the mod only J32 → J66 is used: one 5 MHz clock serves as both reference and SYSREF (derived SYSREF). Remove the card's four corner standoffs before fitting it: there is only about 9 mm to the side wall. Measure the card when it arrives and rescale.

    What stays the same

    The carrier design, the firmware image and the start-up sequence. Every module is identical, so a spare module can replace any other after a skew re-calibration.

    What changes

    The number of sync outputs: the LMK04832 has 14 outputs, enough for 4 modules (4 REF + 4 SYSREF) with spares. 8 modules need 16 outputs, so add buffers. Cable sets, supply size and rack space grow linearly.

    Mixing with the existing ZCU216

    The ZCU216's CLK104 can take REF and SYSREF from the same sync unit, adding 16 DAC channels to the array. It needs its own front end (an RFMC mezzanine), and its outputs will differ slightly from the HamGeek channels, so calibrate it as a separate group.

    DAC-only modules

    If a module only needs RF-DAC outputs, leave the one-bit front ends unpopulated and skip the GTY rework. The 16-DAC option then costs two cards plus two lighter carriers.
