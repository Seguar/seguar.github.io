ESP32-C5 Phased Array

A low-cost 16-element 5.2 GHz digital phased-array research platform built from ESP32-C5 radios, inspired by ESPARGOS and ESP-SDR. The hard problems are synchronization, calibration and getting raw IQ out of the C5 — not the beamforming maths.

Sharing one 48 MHz reference

TopicWhat we know
FrequencyAll 16 LOs derive from one oscillator, so carrier offsets between elements vanish. The absolute ppm error is common to all and harmless.
Static skewBuffer skew becomes a fixed LO phase: 50 ps ≈ 94° at 5.2 GHz. Calibration removes it — but skew drifting with temperature shows up as phase drift.
JitterIn-band reference noise rises by 20·log(5200/48) = 40.7 dB. Jitter common to all elements cancels in relative phase; buffer additive jitter (20–160 fs max ≈ 0.04–0.3°) does not.
InjectionThe C5 design guidelines cover only a 48 MHz ±10 ppm crystal, with no external-oscillator requirements (the datasheet calls XTAL_P/N a crystal or oscillator input). Precedents: the ESP32-S2 guide (oscillator into XTAL_P via ~50 pF), ESPARGOS (40 MHz shared), Espressif’s esp-crab (two C5s, “co-crystal”). C5 modules carry their own crystal.

open Driving XTAL_P of every chip-down C5 from one buffered TCXO is the first clock bench test. The 108th harmonic of 48 MHz falls at 5184 MHz and the 130th of the 40 MHz PARLIO clock at 5200 MHz: series-terminate and slew-limit both clocks, keep them away from the RF lines, and if the spurs show use channel 44 (5210–5230 MHz), the only one of channels 36–48 clear of both clocks' harmonics.

05 · Synchronization

Calibration: one tone, five steps

Details
  1. Lock and fix gain: tune all C5s, disable AGC, set one manual gain index.
  2. Measure the reference: all SPDTs to CAL; step the tone across the capture band (as ESPARGOS One does), one synchronized snapshot per step.
  3. Estimate per element, relative to element 1: gain gₖ and phase φₖ, and delay τₖ from the phase slope across the tone steps.
  4. Measure the scene: all SPDTs to ANT, synchronized snapshots (source muted).
  5. Correct per FFT bin with Cₖ(f) = exp[−j(φₖ + 2πfτₖ)] / gₖ, then combine.

Repeat after every reset, retune or sample-rate change. The tone cannot see anything before the switch: on the tile that is the patch, its probe via and a 1 mm line, the same for all eight elements, so calibrate it once over the air. That step also absorbs the tone's own distribution (splitter, cables, trees): repeat it when a calibration cable moves. Tile B is turned 180°, so its elements carry a fixed 180° term.

06 · Data path

Sixteen receivers make a data problem

what the array produceswhat links and ports carry
Details
  • The C5 samples at up to 80 MS/s internally, but ESP-SDR moves captures out over USB (12 Mbit/s) or UART.
  • A 2-bit PARLIO lane at 40 MHz gives each C5 80 Mbit/s: enough for ~4 MS/s continuous, or for draining full-rate snapshots in a few milliseconds.
  • Eight lanes fill one 16-bit S31 bus (640 Mbit/s) and one Gigabit port per tile — a rate ESP-SDR already pushes through an S31 (≈ 687 Mbit/s on the wire). Continuous raw 80 MS/s from 16 elements stays out of scope.
07 · Data path

Two 8-element tiles and one timing board

Details
  • Chip-down C5 (QFN48): modules carry their own 48 MHz crystal and cannot share a reference.
  • PHY: RTL8211F is the team's choice; Espressif's S31 board uses a YT8531 — check the ESP-IDF driver before layout.
  • The S31 forwards 16-bit words untouched; bit de-interleaving runs on the host.
  • Ethernet and PTP give timestamps and coordination only — sample alignment comes from the shared PCLK, the GO signal and the RF calibration.
08 · Data path

Eight 2-bit streams on one 16-bit bus

  1. the S31 arms all C5s; each captures a snapshot and loads its PARLIO DMA descriptors
  2. each C5 raises READY
  3. the S31 waits until all eight READY lines are high
  4. GO: the S31 enables the PCLK buffer, so all lanes start on one edge
  5. all C5s shift out on the same clock edges
  6. the S31 captures 16-bit words by DMA
  7. every frame starts with a sync word common to all lanes

If one lane slips by a clock, the sync word no longer lines up across the 16 bits and the S31 sees it at once. Bit transposition is cheap on a PC and expensive on the S31 at 40 M words/s.

C5 TRM v1.1 §43: PARLIO ≤ 40 MHz, external TX clock, not an ETM peripheral; a gated external clock may start after TX_START. Lockstep across chips is an inference to test.

  • 5 MS/s is not an ESP-SDR rate, and 8 MS/s (128 Mbit/s) does not fit a 2-bit lane.
  • The C5 has no continuous capture today. Back-to-back 4 MS/s snapshots (4.1 ms each, 3.3 ms to drain) would be near-continuous if capture and transfer can overlap.
  • The C5 analog filter is at least 11 MHz wide: at 4 MS/s out-of-band signals may alias (the S31 stream subsamples without a decimation filter).
  • On the S31, ESP-SDR streams by routing the modem debug bus into PARLIO. The C5 lists MODEM_DIAG0–31 signals, but IQ on them is undocumented — and 16–20 lanes per C5 would not fit the shared bus.

11 · Data path

Plan B: a small FPGA in front of the S31

Details

Switch to Plan B if

  • eight C5 lanes cannot be kept in lockstep
  • the S31 cannot sustain 640 Mbit/s in and out
  • real-time per-element processing (FFT, decimation) is needed

What the FPGA does

  • 8 independent PARLIO receivers
  • elastic FIFO per channel
  • frame alignment, timestamps
  • trigger and switch sequencing
  • calibration correction
  • decimation / FFT
  • later: beamforming

Cost and benefit

  • one more chip, toolchain and HDL work
  • more power and board area
  • the team needs FPGA experience
  • each C5 can run on its own lane clock
  • deterministic, inspectable timing
  • data reduction before Ethernet
  • a path to real-time beamforming

Candidate FPGAs: Lattice ECP5 / iCE40 UltraPlus (open toolchain), Efinix Trion. Optional for Rev 1.

The patch substrate

  • A standard 1.6 mm 4-layer board has only ≈ 0.21 mm between L1 and L2: a 5.2 GHz patch there radiates at most ≈ 18 % of its power on FR-4 (lossless bandwidth ≈ 0.3 %; the loss makes the S11 dip look wider than it is).
  • Rev 1 uses a custom stack with a 1.065 mm core under L1: ≈ 1.6 % (≈ 85 MHz) lossless bandwidth and at most ≈ 53 % efficiency; patch ≈ 17.5 × 13.5 mm, trimmed after a test coupon (≈ 55 MHz per 0.1 of εr).
  • Front: patches only (plus tented through-via pads). Each C5 sits behind its own patch: probe via, 1 mm to the SPDT, 2.5 mm to the C5, the same for all eight. All digital lines run behind the L2 ground.
  • Seam: the ground is cut between tiles, so bridge it continuously and calibrate per element. Tile B is turned 180°, so elements 9–16 carry a fixed 180° term.

To scale for 28.8 mm pitch. Custom 4-layer stack (JLCPCB's standard one has only 0.21 mm under L1). Patch: εr 4.4, tan δ 0.02; lossless BW ≈ 3.77·(εr−1)/εr²·(W/L)·(h/λ₀); efficiency counts dielectric loss only. Prices: team estimates.

14 · Board

Sixteen C5s on one board?

Five ways to put all sixteen C5s on one board with the same 1 GbE + USB (B–F), next to today's 2 × 8 plan (A). All of them use the master board for the clocks, GO and the 5.2 GHz tone.

Verdict. One S31 can run all sixteen (B): the same 80 MS/s snapshots, drained half as fast (≤ 148 instead of ≤ 287 per second). Only the S31 has Gigabit Ethernet (the P4 is 10/100); D, E and F reach ≈ 210 per second but need an FPGA, a switch or every S31 pin. Rev 1: the 2 × 8 tiles (one tile is bench step 4). Rev 2: B if 148 snapshots per second is enough; C only if transmit experiments need full-rate receivers on one board.

  • Total: ≈ 46 signal pins plus 7 for the external flash (GPIO26–32), 53 of the S31's 60 GPIOs. The S31 has four UARTs, so sixteen C5s need the shared bus. A 24-bit expander (TCA6424, $1.79) can take the mux select lines too.
  • The mux and the expander are the only extra parts, about $2. Two tiles need the same kind of clock buffers (a 1:8 and a 1:10 each), so those cost about the same. Prices here are for 1 pc; at 100 pc a 1:8 + 1:10 pair is ≈ $7.45, the 7.5 in the tile cost.
  • 40 Mbit/s per 1-bit lane is 2.5 MS/s of IQ8 before framing. ESP-SDR's rates are 80/40/20/10/8/4 MS/s, so B streams ≈ 2 MS/s (8 MS/s decimated by 4 in the C5).
  • Flashing: esp-serial-flasher runs on an ESP32 host, supports the C5 over UART, and lets the port code supply its own reset and boot callbacks, so they can drive the expander and the mux. It flashes one target at a time: sixteen in a row.
  • The C5 latches GPIO26–28 when it leaves reset (GPIO28 low and GPIO27 high: download mode), so one shared BOOT net is enough. Drive it push-pull from the S31 (sixteen ≈ 45 kΩ internal pull-ups sink ≈ 1.2 mA) and fit no pull-down: a 10 kΩ pull-down, right for one chip, would only reach ≈ 2.6 V against sixteen pull-ups. Pull it low just before the target's EN rises, hold it at least 3 ms, then release, so a C5 that resets later boots normally. Keep GPIO27 high.
  • No hub on the S31's own USB host: with no transaction translator, the C5's full-speed USB works behind a hub only if the host is forced to full speed, and 16 host channels are not enough for 16 C5s plus the hubs. A hub tree to the PC is possible: three 7-port FE2.1 hubs (≈ $2 each) give 19 ports, and esptool flashes all 17 chips in parallel, at the cost of 16 USB pairs to route.
  • C's transmitters need control lines and the shared 48 MHz, no data lanes. Streaming waveforms down to them on the S31's PARLIO TX would need 16 more pins for 2 bits each: 69 of 60. It reaches 60 of 60, with nothing spare, only if the mux select, BOOT and PHY reset move to a 24-bit expander, USB moves to the OTG port and the trigger is dropped. Running PARLIO RX, TX and Ethernet at full rate together is untested. And no public firmware plays raw IQ on the C5: ESP-SDR is receive-only, the documented tone test lists 2.4 GHz channels only, and Wi-Fi frames are started by the MAC, so whether GO can align them is open.
  • PHY: Espressif's S31 board uses a Motorcomm YT8531, and ESPHome supports it on the S31; the ESP-IDF Ethernet page still says RMII only. Prefer the YT8531 unless the RTL8211F driver is checked.

Why not a bigger ESP

  • ESP32-P4: 16-bit PARLIO at 40 MHz like the S31, USB 2.0 HS, but Ethernet is 10/100 (MII/RMII).
  • ESP32-S31: already the widest PARLIO RX (16 lines, 40 MHz) and the only Gigabit MAC in the family.
  • Sixteen C5s at 2 bits is 1.28 Gbit/s: more than one S31's PARLIO and more than one GbE link.
  • The S31's camera (DVP) input is a second 16-bit parallel port with an external pixel clock, so PARLIO plus camera could take 2-bit lanes from all sixteen (F). Pins: 32 data, 2 clocks, 15 Ethernet and 7 flash make 56 of 60, so the resets, BOOT, mux select, GO and PHY reset all move to a 24-bit I²C expander, READY is read through it, and USB goes to the HS OTG port on its own pins. The camera port's maximum clock is not in the datasheet, nobody has shown more than 640 Mbit/s into an S31, and snapshots must sit in its PSRAM while GbE drains them (inference).

Cheap full-rate options

  • One GbE carries ≈ 957 Mbit/s of UDP payload at a 1500-byte MTU (1472 of 1538 bytes of line time), ≈ 932 Mbit/s of IQ after ESP-SDR's packet headers: 58 Mbit/s, a 3.6 MS/s ceiling of IQ8, per element for sixteen. 4 MS/s (1.02 Gbit/s) does not fit, so the rate is cut before the link: resampled in D's FPGA (or cut to 7-bit IQ at 4 MS/s), or decimated in E's C5s to ≈ 3.3 MS/s (10 MS/s ÷ 3).
  • Snapshots in D and E: one 16-element snapshot is 4.2 Mbit and drains over GbE in ≈ 4.5 ms, so ≈ 210 per second. At full lane speed the input runs ≈ 1.1 Mbit ahead of the link per snapshot, about all of the ≈ 1 Mbit of block RAM in a T20 or an ECP5-25, so either clock the lanes at ≈ 29 MHz or add an external RAM (≈ +$2–4).
  • FPGA: Efinix T20 ≈ $12.75, Lattice ECP5-25 ≈ $21.78 (DigiKey, 1 pc); the open LiteEth core has RGMII PHYs for both. It needs a config flash and its own rails, and a small ESP (C3-class) still handles USB and control. D's parts against A: −$19 (two S31s, a PHY, a MagJack), then +$13–22 for the FPGA, ≈ $1–3 for its flash and rails, $0–4 for RAM, ≈ $2 for the small ESP and ≈ $1.5 for the control fan-out: ≈ −$3 to +$15.
  • Switch: the Microchip KSZ9897R (≈ $12.83) has two RGMII MAC ports for the two S31s and integrated PHYs for the uplink. The KSZ9893, RTL8363NB and RTL8367S have one RGMII port each. A MAC-to-MAC link needs a fixed-link PHY driver, as Espressif's KSZ8863 driver does.

16 · Experiments

First experiment: receive beamforming

Δφ per element: −90.0°
broadside (0°) steered
Details

Experiment

  • Place a 5.2 GHz CW or Wi-Fi source in the far field (2D²/λ ≈ 0.9 m for the 163 mm aperture diagonal; use ≥ 1 m).
  • Calibrate with the internal tone, then capture snapshots with the source at known angles.
  • Form beams digitally: y = wᴴx with w = steering vector; scan azimuth and elevation.
  • Estimate angle with conventional beamforming and MUSIC; add a second source for nulling.

Success criterion

  • The measured beam pattern matches the predicted one to within a few dB in the main lobe.
  • The estimated angle repeats after a power cycle and retune — proving that calibration, not luck, produced coherence.

What is actually hard

  • Phase-coherent TX LOs: a shared 48 MHz fixes frequency, not the phase after each PLL lock.
  • Deterministic TX start: normal packet TX is timed by the Wi-Fi MAC, not by us.
  • A raw TX waveform path on the C5 — the ESP-SDR authors say arbitrary TX works but have not released it.
  • The C5 is 1T1R and half-duplex: one chain switches between TX and RX.

Until then

  • Separate roles: 1–2 C5s transmit, the other 14–15 receive coherently.
  • TDM / MIMO: one element transmits at a time, the TX aperture is synthesized afterwards (as in ESPARGOS radar mode).
  • External RF phase shifters after each C5 — more BOM, loss and control lines; not for Rev 1.
  • Regulation: 5150–5250 MHz allows 200 mW EIRP, indoor, in both the EU and Israel, for EN 301 893 RLAN equipment. Sixteen coherent elements add up to 24 dB over one, and custom radar waveforms are probably not covered — do TX experiments cabled, shielded or at low power, on channels 36–48.
